## The one person (not "builders")

**Primary — the bloke with a curved job on the board and a deadline.** A carpenter or formworker who's
been handed a curved wall / curved bench-seat formwork and knows it's the part of the job he's least sure
about. He's done curves the slow way before — kerfing, laminating sheets, freehand jigsaw, bog-and-sand —
and it was *"a good day"* gone (#44), *"a massive slog"* (#55). His real fear isn't price; it's the curve
turning up wrong or not lining up on site *(#21, #37)* and looking *"clunky"* (#5) in front of the builder.
When he quotes a curved job, he prices it high hoping he doesn't win it: *"I would quote $20k for that job
hopefully I don't get it"* (#57).

**Secondary — the architect/specifier who drew the curve.** Believes the curve itself is fine; their
problem is finding a trade who'll build it well without gouging: *"the expense depends on the builder's
willingness… if they are not keen on doing curves, they are likely to charge a lot more"* (#3). They think
in **tolerance** (*"virtually no tolerance in a curved wall"* #1) and **radius-as-cost-dial** (*"as long as
the radius is not too tight… isn't hugely expensive"* #9).

## The 7 truths that repeat across every cluster (high confidence)

1. **"Who even does this?" — sourcing is the first wall.** They literally can't find a supplier: *"Where do
   I get curved drywall?😢"* (#52), *"cannot see anything about being able to have curved… panels?"* (#17).
   **← Craftons' single biggest opening.**
2. **The problem is the *supplier*, not the geometry.** Architects and tradies agree: the curve is doable
   *with the right trade* (#3, #4). Craftons *is* that trade, productised.
3. **Curved = expensive by default** — a belief to reverse: *"much more expensive than traditional box
   buildings"* (#23), *"out of my price range"* (#24), *"anything not standard will be more expensive"* (#6).
4. **The on-site fit nightmare** — parts that don't line up: *"the curves don't match up when the 2 skirts
   meet"* (#21), *"edges don't align"* (#37), wrong colour/downgrade faces delivered (#27). **← the fit guarantee.**
5. **Curved work is slow, dreaded, priced-to-avoid** — *"a good day"* (#44), *"massive slog"* (#55), *"what
   a process"* (#56), *"$20k hopefully I don't get it"* (#57). **← "buy the curve, don't make it."**
6. **The end-state they want is precision** — *"perfect curve every time"* (#50), *"smooth clean radius with
   a perfect finish"* (#51), vs the pain of *"freehand with a jigsaw."* **← CNC precision, in their words.**
7. **Competitors lose on service, not product** — the loudest complaints are lost orders, no comms, slow
   dispatch: *"follow up and follow up… zero communication"* (#32), *"5-9 business days to simply dispatch"*
   (#33). **← differentiate on a tight, communicative process.**

## The avatar (Kong fields — each cited)

### Deepest desires
- Parts that **turn up cut and just fit** — no rework, no bog-and-sand (implied across #21, #37; #4).
- **Speed + being kept informed** — *"what decent local business takes 5-9 business days to simply dispatch
  an order?"* (#33); samples/product that *"arrive very quickly"* (#41), *"delivered on time"* (#42).
- A **perfect finish without the fight** — *"perfect curve every time"* (#50), *"smooth clean radius"* (#51).
- Secretly, to **do the cool curved job** — the appetite is real: *"Shit yeah, I'd love a curved wall"* (#47);
  a Brisbane apprentice: *"this helped a lot!"* (#48).

### Fears & pains
- **It won't fit / won't line up on site** (#21, #37) — the nightmare, in front of the builder.
- **It'll look clunky** — *"hard to make work and if done badly they can look terribly clunky"* (#5).
- **The time-sink** — one curved wall = *"a good day"* (#44); the bench = *"a massive slog"* (#55).
- **Getting caught out** not knowing how — a formwork labourer: *"in case I was ever asked at work"* (#49);
  curved work treated as foreman-only (#46).
- **The telegraphing defect** — *"every curve's last stud shows after plaster… what am I doing wrong?"* (#43).

### Beliefs
- **Curves cost a fortune** (#23, #24, #6, #10).
- **Curves are specialist/foreman-only work** (#46).
- **Steel is the "curves are easy" material** — *"straight or curved it can all be done with steel… charge
  by the metre"* (#18, #20). (What Craftons' ply/formwork/concrete competes against.)
- **The curve is fine — finding the right trade is the hard part** (#3, #4). (Architect belief = our wedge.)

### Objections (what stops the sale)
- *"I knew it was a gamble. I lost."* (#29) — **trust / been-burned.** ← risk reversal answers this word-for-word.
- *"Will it actually fit?"* — *"edges don't align"* (#37); ordered-right-came-wrong (#27).
- **Setup cost** — competitor charges *"$200 setup fee for each size arc"* for curved architraves
  (australianmoulding.com.au). ← undercut with instant, setup-free online pricing.
- **Radius-too-tight = cost cliff** (#9) — needs clear achievable-radius specs to remove the uncertainty.
- **Can I even get it?** — *"where do I get curved drywall?😢"* (#52).

### Buying triggers (the moment they go looking)
- **An existing curve forces the decision** — *"the driveway… is all curved and a square fence might look
  really odd"* (#15).
- **The architect specced a radius / the foreman built one on site** (#46, #12).
- **They've got a curved job to quote** and don't want to eat the risk (#57).

### Emotions
- **Risk-aversion about the outcome** — *"proceed with caution"* / *"clunky"* (#5). Certainty is what they
  secretly want.
- **Dread + resignation** toward the slow curved job — *"what a process"* (#56).
- **Quiet pride** when a curve comes off — and appetite to do more (#47, #48).
