## 2. Three things to fix in the angle before we write a word

Your instinct is right, with one correction and two additions.

**a) The hook is right. The pain underneath it is not the one you said.**
"Architects are specifying curves" is a great opener and it is already a locked hook pattern in
`SOCIAL-VOICE.md`. But "timely and costly on site" is generic. Your own product briefing has a much
sharper pain, in your words, and it is the one the copy has to land on:

> do the maths → draw the radius out on a sheet of ply → cut it with a jigsaw.
> Which is slow, not everyone can do it, workers make mistakes, and a lot of the sheet goes in the bin.

Use the architect line as the setup, then land on the maths/marking/jigsaw. That is the difference
between a generic ad and something a chippy nods at.

**b) Nesting is the strongest thing you own and it has never been in a single piece of content.**
The software nests the parts on the sheet for minimum waste. It is a cost argument, it came from client
feedback, and no competitor makes it. It belongs in this video.

**c) Teach the set-out, not just the clicks.**
`CONTENT-PILLARS.md` has a rule: *"teach for real. If a post only makes sense to someone about to buy,
it belongs in Sell."* A pure click-through of the UI is a Sell post wearing a Teach costume. The fix is
one beat: when arc length and chord length auto-populate, say that out loud as **the set-out numbers**.
That single line is real, transferable knowledge that is useful even to someone who never buys, and it
is what keeps this in the 35% Teach lane instead of the 15% Sell lane.

## 3. Guardrails (read before recording)

### Banned words — Radius Pro copy, no exceptions
**bog-and-sand · bogging · kerfing · kerf bending · curve bending · laminating · wiggle wood · bendy ply
as the problem.** Your own words: *"that's just not related to the job at all."*

> ⚠️ **Doc conflict to fix separately:** `SOCIAL-VOICE.md` still uses "bog-and-sand" in three worked
> examples. That doc was locked 2026-06-23, the product truth is 2026-08-03 and wins. Flagged in §10.

### Bendy ply is a friend, not the enemy
Concreters fix bendy ply **to** the frame Radius Pro cuts. Never position against it.

### Say these (all verified)
- Perfectly draws the radius. No maths, no marking out.
- **Nests the parts on the sheet for minimum waste.**
- Cut on the CNC, so no marking or cutting mistakes.
- **Every part engraved with its part ID** (safe blanket claim; on some jobs it is the radius, on
  others the ID, so "part ID" is the line that is always true).
- Tails: extend a leg 100 to 300mm to meet a straight frame.
- Delivered Australia-wide, FedEx, interstate included.

### Do not say
- **Joiner blocks.** True, but concreters only. This video is aimed at carpenters framing curved walls.
- Any waste percentage. We do not have the number yet, so "minimal waste" / "not paying for offcuts" is
  as far as we can honestly go. (Getting that figure is still the highest-value open answer in `STATUS.md`.)
- Anything about sending us a plan or a CAD file. The Drive golden rule stands. Self-serve only.

### Use a real job, not a toy demo
Order data says the product is **structural curved wall plates**: 90mm width dominant, 17mm formply,
radii routinely 1.7m to 10m, quantities of 16 to 60 parts. The rendered ads got binned for showing a
900mm decorative arc. **Build a real multi-metre wall on screen**, not a small pretty curve.

## 4. The reusable structure (your three pillars, formalised)

Use this template for every video from here. It is the spine, the script below is one instance of it.

| Pillar | Job | Length | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | Earn the next 3 seconds. Name a pain they had this month. | 0:00–0:12 | The pain must be theirs, in their words, not ours. No brand yet. |
| **Solution** | Show the mechanism working. Teach one real thing inside it. | 0:12–0:55 | Screen does the proving. At least one beat must be useful even if they never buy. |
| **Benefit** | The payoff, in outcomes on site. | 0:55–1:15 | Time, mistakes, waste, cost. Then one soft CTA. Never two. |

**Hook discipline:** first line is the hook. No "g'day, welcome back", no intro, no name. Start on the
problem, mid-thought. You can introduce yourself in episode five once people care.

## 5. Format & production spec

| | |
|---|---|
| **Aspect** | 9:16, 1080x1920 |
| **Layout** | Radius Pro fills the frame. Lee PIP bottom right, about 26 to 30% of frame width, rounded corners, subtle shadow |
| **Screen** | Desktop screen capture of the live configurator |
| **Presenter** | iPhone, shot separately (see below) |
| **Runtime** | 70 to 80 seconds primary, 30 second lean cut for the second post |
| **Audio** | Lee's voice, close mic. No music bed, or something very low |
| **Captions** | Burned in. Feed autoplays silent, so the video has to work muted |

**PIP corner is the right call here** and it is a deliberate change from the earlier retarget brief
(which put the screen in the top third and the face in the bottom two thirds). That split is correct
when the *face* is the story. In a how-to the *screen* is the story, so the face shrinks to a corner.
Your instinct matches the format.

### The one production trap: aspect ratio
A 16:9 browser window dropped into a 9:16 frame leaves two enormous dead bands and the UI ends up
postage-stamp small on a phone. Fix it at capture, not in the edit:

1. **Resize the browser window to portrait** before recording, roughly 1200 x 1600.
2. **Zoom the browser to 150 to 175%** so the field labels are legible at thumb size.
3. Keep the fields you are talking about and the live preview both on screen. If they will not both
   fit, capture wide and let the edit push in on each field as you name it.
4. Test one screenshot on your phone before you record the whole thing. If you cannot read the
   labels at arm's length, it is too small.

### Shoot it in two passes, not one
This is your second time to camera. Do not try to talk and drive the app at the same time.

- **Pass 1 — the screen.** Record the configurator build cleanly, no audio, no pressure. Redo it until
  the mouse movement is calm. Slow and deliberate beats fast and jittery.
- **Pass 2 — the face.** Play the screen capture back on the monitor and talk to the iPhone while it
  runs. You get natural eyeline, correct pacing, and if you fluff a line you re-record one line, not
  the whole video.
- Clap once at the top of pass 2 for a sync point.
- **Do not memorise the script.** Learn the beats in §6 and say them your way. The rough version in
  your own words beats the polished version in mine, every time.
