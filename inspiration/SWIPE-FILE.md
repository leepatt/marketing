# Brand teardowns — the swipe file

_Six teardowns, researched 2026-06-19 with receipts. The evidence base behind `CONTENT-PILLARS.md`.
Brands chosen by Lee: Gozney, July, BuildPass (nominated) + The B1M (format) + Hommey, Bellroy._

> Method: real observed content + published analyses. Feed-% are estimates from sampled posts
> (IG/TikTok block full scraping), flagged as directional, not audited.

---

## The cross-brand pattern (the synthesis)

1. **Sell is the smallest lane.** Gozney ~8%, July soft/rare, Bellroy sells but sell-last. Value-first is what the best do.
2. **The product demonstrating itself IS the content.** Bellroy wallet-slim, July "one feature one shot," Gozney cheese-pull, Bellroy x-ray cutaway → for Craftons = Tia close-ups + rendered configurator. Highest-leverage, cheapest move.
3. **Education-as-a-show.** Gozney "How To Pizza" (named, numbered, evergreen) — the single most copyable format.
4. **Customer/builder is the hero, not the brand.** Hommey "by you", BuildPass named-builder case studies, Gozney credited UGC. BuildPass proves the inverse: inside-out feed → dead engagement (~0.5%). Builders trust builders.
5. **Restraint = looking big.** July & Bellroy look national on small budgets via discipline (calm captions, consistent palette, sound design not meme audio). = "big company not small workshop", for free.
6. **Consistency/library depth beats chasing virality** (Gozney's per-post engagement is modest; the win is the system).

### What it means for Craftons' pillars
- **Teach** ✅ strongest. **Inspire** ✅ reframed to builder-as-hero. **Sell** ✅ kept small (~10–15%).
- **Entertain** ❌ not a standalone lane — none of these brands run comedy/tier-lists. "Entertainment" = product-beauty shown gorgeously → becomes the **Craft Macro** format, not a pillar.
- Operate as **named repeatable formats**, not abstract topics: How To Curve / Craft Macro / Built with Craftons / Watch It Configure + Spec Drops / How This Curve Was Built (animated flagship).

## 1. Gozney (pizza ovens) — @gozney ~390k
**The lifestyle-led playbook. #1 benchmark.**
- **Pillars:** food-porn/craft beauty ~35% · education "How To Pizza" ~25% · creator/ambassador collabs ~20% · lifestyle/gathering ~12% · product/sell ~8%.
- **Signature formats:** cheese-pull/fire macro · numbered "How To Pizza" episodic series · ambassador takeover · credited UGC regram · recipe-beyond-pizza.
- **Hooks/voice:** open visually (fire/cheese), not text; warm plain captions; "Learn how to: +" bullet lists; soft CTAs ("full episode on GozneyTV, link in bio"); branded hashtags #HowToPizza #CookDifferent.
- **Why it works:** sell the outcome not the object · turn the category into a curriculum · borrow credibility + outsource production via creators.
- **Reality check:** per-post engagement modest vs followers (713/589 likes on 390k) — win = consistency + library depth, not virality.
- **Receipts:** How To Pizza Ep.01 reel (instagram.com/gozney/reel/C58m0m7iuux/) · holiday credited-UGC reel (.../reel/DDKZCi0IDgc/) · How To Pizza hub (us.gozney.com/pages/how-to-pizza) · Academy (us.gozney.com/blogs/academy) · ambassadors (gozney.com/blogs/brand-ambassadors).
- **Steal:** (1) "How To Curve" named evergreen education series. (2) "cheese-pull" = fetishize the material (ply flexing, light on a finished radius) — Tia macro, sound-led. (3) builder/architect collabs + credited project footage = your UGC engine.

## 2. July (premium luggage, AU) — @july ~235k
**Premium DTC craft. Restraint = premium.**
- **Pillars (est.):** product-hero/feature storytelling ~35% · personalisation ~15% · colour/collection drops ~15% · aspirational travel ~20% · packing how-to + UGC ~15%. (AU creative skews aspirational; US skews functional.)
- **Signature formats:** colour-as-character drop · mechanism close-up reel (one feature doing its clever thing) · personalisation reveal · pack-with-me · destination scene.
- **Hooks/voice:** open on the object/transformation; clean confident design-mag calm; short declaratives, almost no emoji on IG; soft CTAs ("Personalise your July").
- **Why it works:** one feature, one shot · colour/variant as recurring news (novelty from one SKU) · premium = restraint.
- **Receipts:** "Once you pack a July trunk…" reel (instagram.com/reel/DVVMgRJkQoh/) · luggage-tag personalisation (.../july/p/DAiBUcERn4o/) · Brown collection drop (hypebeast.com/2025/3/july-brown-collection…) · strategy (modernretail.co; smartcompany.com.au; righthookdigital.com/case-study/july/).
- **Steal:** (1) "one feature, one shot" macro reels — Tia. (2) variant/spec-as-news calendar (each radius/thickness/profile/feature = a "drop"). (3) configurator "personalisation reveal" carousels (rendered + AI). **Caution:** skip aspirational lifestyle — trades won't bite; lead with craft/mechanism; restraint is the copyable bit.

## 3. BuildPass (construction SaaS, AU) — @buildpass_ ~4.4k
**Same audience as Craftons. A negative example.**
- **Pillars (est.):** customer case studies ~35–40% · events/expo ~20–25% · product explainers ~15–20% · milestones/culture ~10–15% · PR ~5–10%. **Company-centric, not audience-first.**
- **Signature formats:** talking-head case-study reel (flagship) · expo recap · milestone reel · founder "caught up with" · feature highlight.
- **Hooks/voice:** curiosity + aspiration ("Ever wondered how the best… are levelling up?"); medium-long warm emoji-sprinkled captions; soft CTAs ("book a demo, link in bio"); friendly-startup voice, NOT builder slang.
- **Why it works / fails:** receipts beat claims (named builder on camera = credible) ✅ · events manufacture "big company" perception ✅ · BUT too inside-out → low engagement (16–40 likes / 4.4k = ~0.4–1%). No value-give = no saves/shares. ❌
- **Receipts:** FORA case study (instagram.com/buildpass_/reel/DBNFmZVvySW/) · Jardon case study, their best (.../reel/Cxb2FBuPp7X/) · Sydney Build Expo recap (.../reel/C6ftFgqruQc/) · "BuildPass turned 3" (.../reel/DAE2g49BNmH/).
- **Audience insight:** this audience engages with a real named peer giving a concrete outcome; barely engages with brand self-congratulation.
- **Steal:** (1) "named builder, real outcome" case study — but make it value-first (lead with the builder's win). (2) expo/"big stage" framing → fake scale cheaply via AI wide b-roll + Tia close-ups. (3) **out-teach them** — own the save-worthy how-to lane they leave empty (configurator demos + carousels).

## 4. The B1M (construction video media) — YouTube ~4M
**Format reference, not a competitor.**
- **Format anatomy (8–12 min narrated doc):** 0–20s cold-open hook (bold claim/number) → stakes+thesis → (sponsor) → problem/engineering challenge → the solve/build → payoff/zoom-out. Also runs 1–5 min silent captioned vertical "social cuts" from the same footage.
- **How it's made:** blend of **developer/contractor-supplied footage & CGI renders (often FREE — the cheat)** + licensed stock + own shoots + disclosed AI. Real moat = clean motion-graphic **diagrams**. Single narrator (Fred Mills), tight journalism, licensed cinematic music. Cost = bespoke graphics, script/research, shoots, music.
- **Hook/title pattern:** [Why/Building] + [superlative or $figure] + [named place]. One concrete number does the work.
- **Why it works:** one number = the whole hook · make the invisible legible with diagrams · story spine (problem→tension→solve→payoff), one trusted voice.
- **Receipts:** Thinnest Skyscraper 111W57 (theb1m.com/video/building-the-worlds-thinnest-skyscraper) · Billionaires' Row Half Empty (.../why-new-yorks-billionaires-row-is-half-empty) · Australia's $3.6BN Megaproject (.../building-australias-3-6bn-megaproject) · Crown Sydney curved tower (.../building-a-2bn-skyscraper-from-home).
- **Cheapest executable version for Craftons:** DON'T clone photoreal doc (their cheat = free developer footage you lack; AI-faking real buildings looks uncanny + risky). **Change format, not budget → fully-animated/illustrated explainer (Kurzgesagt/Vox/Practical Engineering mode).** Keep B1M's story spine + hook discipline; drop footage dependency. Recipe for "How This Curve Was Built": ~80% reusable motion-graphic kit · configurator render = your free "CGI flythrough" · Tia macro = texture layer (20–30s, what AI can't fake) · AI for stylized/illustrative only (disclosed, never fake real landmarks) · CC footage as garnish · one consistent narrator · cheap music sub (Epidemic/Artlist ~$15–30/mo) · chop into vertical social cuts. **Bluntly:** front-loaded labour; eps 1–3 slow while building the kit, 4+ fast. Aim "clear, branded, informative," not cinematic.

## 5. Hommey (homewares, AU) — @hommey ~280k
**Community machine. Translate mechanics, not voice.** Founder: Justin Kestelman.
- **Pillars (est.):** product/colour drops ~40% · community "by you" UGC ~20% · lifestyle styling ~20% · founder/brand story ~10% · giveaways/launches ~10%.
- **Signature formats:** drop/restock announcement (scarcity) · UGC creator feature reel · colour-grid "pick your shade" · lifestyle mood reel · launch+giveaway combo.
- **Hooks/voice:** conversational second-person ("Wake up babe: striped robes are back 💖"); short warm captions; recurring "This is Hommey by you"; soft scarcity CTAs. Founder personality lives in earned media/LinkedIn, brand voice = personality on-grid.
- **Why it works:** make the customer the protagonist · manufactured scarcity tied to real demand · ownable visual signature (colour) + best-friend voice.
- **Receipts:** "robes restocked… Hommey by you" (instagram.com/hommey/p/C9lw99TTqvQ/) · "Wake up babe…" UGC reel (.../reel/C7fp9JJPLuM/) · TikTok 2000% US spike Dec24→Mar25 (insideretail.com.au, "why-australian-home-goods-brand-hommey…").
- **Steal:** (1) **"Built with Craftons"** = trade "by you" — repost real credited jobs (HIGH translate). (2) drop/restock → "spec/capability drop" (MEDIUM — reframe FOMO as new capability, no fake urgency for B2B). (3) comparison/education that earns third-party reach ("curved ply vs kerf-cutting vs 2-sheet bend"). **Caveat:** Craftons = trades not consumers — bin the cutesy "babe" voice, keep builder-to-builder + spec-literate.

## 6. Bellroy (carry goods, AU) — @bellroy
**Make a functional product desirable. Demo = sell.**
- **Pillars (est.):** product-as-hero demos ~35% · lifestyle "where you go" ~25% · craft/BTS ~15% · comparison/problem-agitate ~15% · community/light entertainment ~10%.
- **Signature formats:** stop-motion product demo (signature) · x-ray/cutaway feature reveal · before/after comparison (fat wallet → slim) · close-up detail clip (no narration, on-screen labels) · lifestyle context shot.
- **Hooks/voice:** visual-first (open on the problem or the satisfying mechanism); short calm benefit-led captions (email subjects ~21 chars); soft product-led CTAs.
- **Why it works:** make the function the spectacle · show the invisible (x-ray) · problem-first, premium-finish (cheap problem, expensive-looking solution; lighting/restraint = "big brand").
- **Receipts:** Slim Your Wallet stop-motion series (bellroy.com/explore/slim-your-wallet; YouTube v=IvkAjZd8rjE) · "Where stop-motion starts" journal (bellroy.com/journal/where-stop-motion-starts) · comparison swipe file (swiped.co/file/bellroy-advertising/).
- **Steal:** (1) "watch it work" demo as the sell unit → configurator build + Tia ply-bend close-up with clean labels (radius/sheet/lead time). (2) x-ray/cutaway "show the invisible" → curved-ply cross-section / flat→curve transform with dimension overlay (AI-suited). (3) before/after problem-agitate carousel (site-built formwork = slow/wasteful → Craftons = precise). **Caution:** don't replicate stop-motion technique (years/expensive); steal the principle, execute with configurator render + AI + Tia.
