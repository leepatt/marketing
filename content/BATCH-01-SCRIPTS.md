# Batch 01 — ten scripts for Jake

**Status:** draft for Lee/Jake. **Date:** 2026-09-12.
**Source:** every one of these comes from a real enquiry in `research/market-intel/`. Nothing invented.
**Test design:** source and format held constant (Jake, talking head). **Hook type and opening frame
are what vary.** That's the one thing batch 01 measures.

---

## ⚠️ Read this first — a doctrine conflict to settle

`CONTENT-PILLARS.md` brand law #1 says:

> **"Big company, never small workshop. No workshop tours, no phone-in-the-shed footage.**
> Process/beauty content is allowed *only* studio-clean or rendered."

That was locked when the plan was Tia-shot polished content. The current plan — Jake, phone, volume,
fortnightly batches — departs from it. **Someone has to decide which wins**, and it should be Lee, not
a quiet override.

**Three ways to resolve it:**

| Option | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A. Amend the law** (recommended) | Change it to *"never looks small"* rather than *"never workshop"*. Phone footage is fine if framing, background and audio are controlled and consistent. A tidy talking head against a clean workshop wall reads big. A cluttered bench with echo reads small. |
| B. Keep the law as written | Jake films against a plain wall or a clean studio-style setup. No workshop visible. Cheaper to shoot, loses the machine advantage. |
| C. Suspend it for the 8-week test | Explicitly time-boxed, revisit at week 8 with data. |

**These scripts assume Option A** — controlled framing, workshop visible but deliberately composed.
If Lee picks B, the shot lists still work; just change the background.

**The non-negotiable either way:** consistent framing across all ten. Same spot, same distance, same
light. That consistency is what makes a phone look professional, and it's the actual content of law #1.

---

## The shoot

**One session. Ten clips. About 90 minutes.**

Set up once and don't move: same position, same framing, same light for all ten. Lav mic on, always.
Shoot each clip twice — Jake's second take is nearly always the better one and the editor is told to
prefer it.

**Framing:** waist-up, Jake slightly off-centre, background falling away behind him. Leave headroom —
remember the top 270px is unsafe on Reels.

**Before you start, collect the B-roll** (screen recordings and photos, five minutes each):
- A plan view in CAD next to a flattened developed view
- A DXF opening cleanly, versus a messy Revit/IFC export
- The Woodtron cutting a curve (side-on, tight)
- Finished curved plates stacked
- A Plan Scan upload, screen-recorded
- Two ply edges side by side, one clean, one furry

## 1. The flattened plan view

**Tests:** problem-statement hook · opening on the CAD screen, not Jake's face · 45–60s

> **Hook:** "Your plan view isn't cuttable. That's not your fault, but it's why the quote's slow."

**The substance** (this is the biggest repeated friction in the whole inbox — it kills jobs before
price is even discussed):
- A curve drawn in plan is a curve seen from above. Flat on the page, not flat in reality.
- To cut it, someone has to unwrap it into a developed view — the true shape, laid flat.
- That unwrapping currently happens in email, over days, sometimes dragging the architect in.
- Send a DXF or DWG with the actual shape and the job moves the same day.

**Shots:** open on the CAD plan view · cut to Jake at "that's not your fault" · back to screen for the
flattened version side by side · end on Jake.

**Caption:** Plan view is the shape from above. The cut needs the shape laid flat. Two different
drawings, and the second one is the one that gets your job moving.

## 2. What the architect sent versus what we can cut

**Tests:** POV hook · opening on Jake · 30–45s

> **Hook:** "Architect sends an IFC. Good luck cutting that."

**The substance** — verbatim from an architect in the inbox: *"Our Revit software does not have a
single developed view… we can export and provide an IFC 3d model if this helps."* It doesn't, on its own.
- A 3D model holds the whole building. A cutter needs one flat profile.
- Best case: DXF or DWG of the developed view.
- Second best: the radius and the chord. Two numbers often beat a 40MB model.
- Worst case: a screenshot. Can't scale it, can't trust it.

**Shots:** Jake to camera · cut to a fly-around of a messy 3D model · cut to a clean two-line DXF.

**Caption:** Three ways to send us a curve, best to worst. Number two surprises people.

---

## 3. Just send the radius

**Tests:** bold-claim hook · opening on Jake · under 20s

> **Hook:** "You don't need the drawings. Just the radius."

**The substance** — lifted almost word for word from a real reply: *"Even just the circle radius will
allow us to quote."*
- Internal radius and the arc length. That's a quote.
- Full drawings matter for cutting, not for pricing.
- Stop waiting on the architect to price a job.

**Shots:** Jake, one continuous take. No b-roll. Deliberately the shortest in the batch.

**Caption:** Internal radius and arc length is enough for a number. Everything else can wait.

## 4. What a lineal metre actually costs

**Tests:** number hook · opening on stacked plates · 45–60s

> **Hook:** "Forty lineal metres of curved plate. Seven sheets of formply."

**The substance** — real job, Perfetto Homes:
- 17mm formply, plates 90mm wide, 40 lineal metres, seven sheets.
- Nesting is what decides your price. Tight curves waste sheet; gentle ones nest close.
- A lineal-metre rate is a fair ask and we'll give one, but the radius changes it.

**Spec stamp:** `40 LM · 90mm · 17mm formply`

**Shots:** open on stacked finished plates · Jake for the explanation · screen recording of the nest.

**Caption:** Why nobody quotes curved work by the metre without asking the radius first.

> ⚠️ **Check the numbers with Lee before filming.** These are real and they must stay real.

## 5. Two layers of 6mm, or something thicker

**Tests:** question hook · opening on Jake · 45–60s

> **Hook:** "Taller wall than you've done before. Does the bendy ply need to change?"

**The substance** — a real question from a structural concreter: *"As far as thickness of bendy ply
we'd welcome some feedback… previously we've used 2 x 6mm but these walls are higher."*
- Two layers of 6mm bends easily and holds a fair radius.
- Height changes the pressure, not just the curve.
- Tight radius wants thin and laminated. Taller pour wants stiffness.
- Jake gives the actual recommendation.

**Shots:** Jake · cut to two ply edges side by side · back to Jake.

**Caption:** Height changes the answer, not just the radius. Worth a think before you order.

> ⚠️ **Jake supplies the real recommendation.** Don't script a number he wouldn't say on site.

---

## 6. Stagger your joints

**Tests:** problem-statement hook · opening on finished plates · 30–45s

> **Hook:** "Doubled plates with the joints lined up. That's a flat spot waiting to happen."

**The substance** — from a builder's enquiry: *"doubled layer with your curved plated joints
positioned/staggered."* He already knew. Plenty don't.
- Two layers, joints stacked, and the curve goes flat right there.
- Stagger them and the curve stays true.
- We stagger by default. Ask if you want them somewhere specific.

**Shots:** open tight on a staggered joint · Jake · back to the joint.

**Caption:** Small detail, shows up badly in the finish.

---

## 7. Can this even be cut

**Tests:** question hook · opening on Jake · 30–45s

> **Hook:** "Everyone asks if it's achievable. It nearly always is."

**The substance** — recurring enquiry: *"wondering if this is still possible to be CNC cut if CAD files
of plans are sent?"*
- Compound curves, tight radii, curve running into a straight tail. All fine.
- The limit is the file, not the machine.
- What actually stops a job: geometry nobody can unwrap.

**Shots:** Jake · cut to a genuinely awkward job being cut · back to Jake.

**Caption:** The machine is rarely the limit. The drawing usually is.

---

## 8. How long it actually takes

**Tests:** number hook · opening on the machine · 30–45s

> **Hook:** "Two weeks, worst case. Usually less."

**The substance** — real quoted turnaround on a curved architrave job was max two weeks.
- The clock starts when we have a cuttable file, not when you email.
- That gap is where most of the wait lives.
- Get the file right and you claw back days.

**Spec stamp:** `2 WEEKS MAX · FROM CUTTABLE FILE`

**Shots:** open on the machine running · Jake · end on finished parts.

**Caption:** Lead time starts at the file, not the enquiry. Worth knowing when you're planning a pour.

---

## 9. Matching an existing profile

**Tests:** POV hook · opening on a profile close-up · 45–60s

> **Hook:** "You've got one architrave left and a house to match."

**The substance** — real joiner enquiry: *"would you be able to match close or similar to the design
I've attached?"* Job ran 18mm MDF, 60mm wide, not the 67mm he first asked for.
- Send a photo with a rule in shot, or a section, or the offcut itself.
- We cut to your profile, not a catalogue one.
- Widths matter. 60 is not 67 and it shows on a door.

**Spec stamp:** `60mm · 18mm MDF`

**Shots:** tight on a profile · Jake · cut to the profile drawn in CAD.

**Caption:** Send the offcut. Beats a description every time.

---

## 10. Snapping to the architect's lines

**Tests:** bold-claim hook · opening on the screen · 45–60s

> **Hook:** "We snap to the architect's lines. The curve is exactly their curve."

**The substance** — this is Lee's own line from the inbox and it's the best sentence in there. It
answers the fear underneath every curved enquiry: *will it actually match the drawing?*
- We build off their geometry, not a redraw.
- No eyeballing, no interpretation, no "close enough".
- Which is why the file matters so much.

**Shots:** screen recording of snapping to CAD lines · Jake for the promise · cut to the cut part
sitting on the drawing.

**Caption:** The curve on site should be the curve on the drawing. That's the whole job.
