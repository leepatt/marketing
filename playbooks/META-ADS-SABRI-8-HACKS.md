# Meta Ads Playbook: Sabri's 8 Hacks (reusable template)

> Source: Sabri Suby, "I've spent over $300M on Meta ads, these are the 8 hacks I wish I knew sooner."
> This is the in-depth, reusable version. Use it to launch any new Craftons Meta campaign the same way
> the Radius Pro TOF campaign was built (see the worked example at the bottom, and the live record in
> `campaigns/meta/radius-pro-tof/`).
>
> House rules for everything in here: builder-to-builder voice, no em dashes or en dashes ever, and
> only claim what is true about the product (for Craftons: we CUT curved plates, we do not bend).

---

## The one idea the whole video rests on

**You are not selling your product in the ad. You are funnelling as many applicable people as you can
to a landing page, as cheaply as possible, with native creative the algorithm can read.** Everything
below serves that. Selling happens on the page and in the follow-up, not in the ad.

Three levers, in order of leverage:
1. **Creative** (the one thing you control, and the thing that targets now). Spend your time here.
2. **Copy** (long-form, specific, native). It gives the algorithm the context to find the buyer.
3. **Settings** (broad, simple). Stop trying to outsmart the algorithm. Feed it and get out of the way.

Judge all of it on **net cash**, never on a ROAS percentage or Meta's own "opportunity score".

## The 8 hacks, in depth

### Hack 1: Statics beat video, win on volume
**What he says.** Since Meta launched its "Andromeda" AI, the account is a hungry panda: it needs an
absurd, constant flood of fresh creative or ads fatigue. Video at that volume is almost impossible to
produce, even with a full team. Statics are far cheaper and quicker, you can run thousands, and the
Facebook algorithm is biased toward statics because it can show more of them per session (more ad
impressions per user is more revenue for Meta, so it favours the format). His own "down and dirty
native statics" outperform VSSLs that took two and a half months to produce.

**Why it works.** Volume defeats fatigue, and cheap statics let you test far more angles per dollar.

**Craftons application.** Statics are the paid workhorse. Real job photos, configurator renders, and
spec or number cards are near-infinite raw material. Carousels count as statics and tell a story well.

**The action.** Block **one hour per week** to cut fresh creative from the current winning offer. Do
not polish. Raw and native beats produced.

**Pitfall.** Waiting to make one "hero" ad. Make a batch. Ship, read, cut more.

---

### Hack 2: The one-keyword hack (the identity trigger)
**What he says.** Andromeda reads your image, copy, offer, and landing page to decide who to serve.
So to reach a specific group, you put that group's word IN the creative and body copy. Take your best
winning ad, look at the 20 percent of clients who are 80 percent of revenue, find the niches they sit
in, and drop the niche word into the headline or body. "462 leads per week" becomes "462 dental leads"
or "lawyer leads" or "property investment leads". You duplicate the winner and insert the keyword. It
is a category word, an identity trigger, that both speaks to that person and tells the algorithm who to
go find. It drops cost per lead and opens audience pockets a generic ad cannot reach.

**Why it works.** The creative is the targeting. The identity word is a signal to both the human and
the machine.

**Craftons application.** Our niches are the trades and the job types. One winning ad, cloned by
swapping a single word: Concreters, Landscapers, Formworkers, Builders, plus job types like curved
bench seats, curved garden beds, curved wall plates, curved stairs, arched doorways.

**The action.** Keep the winner intact. Duplicate it, change ONE word (the trade or job type), launch.
Do not rewrite the ad. Sabri: it "should pretty much read exactly the same."

**Pitfall.** Writing a whole new ad per niche. That is not the hack. Swap the word.

---

### Hack 3: The winning-format clone (never ride one winner)
**What he says.** Amateurs find a winner, kill everything else, and ride it until it fatigues and dies,
resting all their performance on one ad. Instead, feed the winner to AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, his
Kong) and spit out hundreds of variations. His prompt premise: "read this ad and be the author. Write
it so that not one in a hundred people could tell it was written by a different person." Then: "now
write it for 25 year old women, now for a 30 year old woman." It reads the same, with small things that
relate to that person. Do body copy first, then headlines, then creative. Put the winners in a CBO and
let Meta pivot spend. The ads that get no spend but you have high conviction in go into a **zombie
campaign** (their own ad set), which typically juices out another 20 percent of winners.

**Why it works.** You compound one winner into a fleet instead of betting on a single fragile ad.

**Craftons application.** Once one trade or job type wins, clone its structure across the others, keep a
swipe file of winners, and relaunch the good-but-starved ads in their own ad set.

**The action.** After the first read, take the winner, generate variations, run in a CBO, zombie the
high-conviction no-spend ones.

**Pitfall.** Doing this before you have a proven winner. Clone the winner, not a guess.

### Hack 4: Do not make ads look like ads
**What he says.** People hate ads (hundreds of millions run ad blockers). The instinct to make an ad
"pop and hijack attention" backfires. Do the opposite: run the content people already consume natively
on the platform. He runs a 46 minute, minimally edited YouTube video that has millions of organic views
AS an ad, because it already proved it bangs organically. Then he pours gasoline on it. If you do not
have a viral asset, study the native content in your niche. Burner-account trick: make a fresh TikTok
or Instagram account, follow all the niche influencers and pages, like their content, and the most
popular niche content rises to the top of your feed. Make that, run it as an ad, and it will beat your
current best.

**Why it works.** Native content earns attention instead of triggering ad-blindness.

**Craftons application.** Real curved-job content, clean. No wordmark lockups, no CTA pills, no
catalogue panels, no designed taglines slapped over a photo. The photo (or the real before/after) is
the content. If it looks designed, it is dead on arrival.

**The action.** Lead with a real job. Strip all ad chrome. The words live in the caption, not on the
image. Ideally post it organically first, and boost what already performed.

**Pitfall (we made this one).** Building slick brand cards and calling them native. If a tradie can
tell it is an ad at a glance, start over.

### Hack 5: Broad targeting with super-specific creative
**What he says.** Two common mistakes: interest-stacking (ultra-layered targeting) and pure geo YOLO.
Both miss the point: the creative is the targeting now, and the more context you give Meta, the better
it finds people. Most people run short basic copy, so Meta's context window for the ad is tiny, which
limits how many of the right people it can find. Use **long-form copy** that gives the algorithm a huge
amount of data about exactly who you are targeting. Stop trying to outsmart the highest-paid engineers
on earth whose only job is to put your ad in front of the right people. Feed it and get out of the way.

**Why it works.** Long, specific copy is a richer targeting signal than any interest checkbox.

**Craftons application.** Run broad AU with empty interests. Let long-form, trade-specific copy (the
trade, the job types, the real pain, the real process) do the targeting.

**The action.** Duplicate your best ad, remove all interest targeting, pick the country, and run it side
by side against an interest-stacked version. Read CPAs at 7 days. Broad usually wins.

**Pitfall.** Short punchy copy. It starves the algorithm. Go long and specific.

---

### Hack 6: Match the ad to the landing page (scent)
**What he says.** Most people spend 90 percent of effort on ads and 10 percent on the landing page,
which is backwards. If there is no congruence between what someone clicked and what they land on, they
bounce: you can have great CTR and cheap clicks but CPA and conversion rate in the toilet because the
scent is broken. Meta is the best split tester on earth, and headlines are seen roughly a thousand
times more than the landing page, so you get far more significant data on which headline works. Run 20
to 40 headline variants, find the winner, and mirror it on the landing page (the H1, the sub-headline,
and the lead-in). That alone usually lifts conversion 15 to 20 percent. Keep at least three split tests
running at all times.

**Why it works.** Continuity of message from click to page keeps people in the flow to purchase.

**Craftons application.** The winning ad headline must greet them at the top of the builder page it
points to. If the ad says "curves cut to the exact radius", the radius-online page should open with
that promise.

**The action.** Test many headlines. Take the winner and put it on the page hero. Recheck conversion.

**Pitfall.** Launching without checking the page opening matches the ad promise.

---

### Hack 7: Retarget with a different offer
**What he says.** They did not buy because THAT offer was not right for them, so do not just hit them
with the same ad again. If you have multiple products or services, retarget the bouncers with a
different offer (his protein-powder buyer retargeted with creatine went from a 2 to 3x return up to a
6x). The deeper funnel: TOF ad, then landing page, then a high-value content offer (a free report),
and crucially you outbound-call every person who downloads it to mine their pains, fears, hopes, and
why they did not buy. That intel builds: (a) objection-handling ads, (b) proof and testimonial carousel
ads, (c) a CBO of your other offers, and (d) a call or audit that delivers value in itself whether or
not they buy.

**Why it works.** A product spread lets you re-approach the same person from a different angle, and the
calls feed market intelligence back into everything.

**Craftons application.** Bounced off Radius Pro, retarget with the Formwork Builder, the Architrave
Builder, or a curved-jobs field guide. This is Phase 2, after the TOF test reads.

**The action.** Set up a retargeting CBO of the other products for site visitors who did not convert.

**Pitfall.** Retargeting with the identical offer they already ignored.

---

### Hack 8: Track net free cash flow, not ROAS percentage
**What he says.** This separates people who run ads from people who run businesses. A guy at a 40x
return would not spend more. When pushed to spend more, his ROAS percentage dropped (say 15 down to 11)
and cost per lead rose, but he made more actual money in the 30 day period. The ROAS percentage going
down does not matter. What matters is net cash. Optimise blended ROAS at the business level, not per ad
account, and really optimise net free cash flow. Find your break-even ROAS (the most you can spend to
acquire a customer) and keep feeding the beast toward that number, scaling as hard as you can, even if
ROAS drops from 10 to 5, because the difference between spending $1k a month and $100k a month is far
more money at the end. Confidence comes from data, so protect data integrity and block 3 hours a month
to look at the numbers yourself, not through an analyst or media buyer.

**Why it works.** Percentages are vanity. Cash pays wages. Scaling a lower percentage over a much
larger base makes more money.

**Craftons application.** Average curved job is around $1,450. A $30 to $60 lead that lands those is a
scale signal, not a percentage to protect. Ignore Meta's Opportunity Score.

**The action.** Define break-even. Scale winners toward it in steps. Book the monthly numbers review.

**Pitfall.** Capping spend to protect a ROAS percentage while leaving cash on the table.

## The repeatable launch workflow (the template)

Run these in order for any new campaign.

1. **Pick the winning offer and a real, native asset.** Start from a proven product and a real job
   (photos or a short clip). Real footage leads. AI only extends, and only when nothing real exists.
2. **Build native creative (Hack 1 and 4).** A clean real photo or a before/after carousel. No wordmark
   lockup, no CTA pill, no catalogue panel, no tagline on the image. Carousel card headlines should
   BUILD a short story (what, how, result), each specific and useful, never flat labels.
3. **Write long-form copy (Hack 5) using the copy template below.** One human story, specific, in the
   trade's voice, that funnels rather than sells. First line earns the "more" expand.
4. **Identity-clone across niches (Hack 2 and 3).** Keep the winner, swap ONE word (the trade or job
   type). Do not rewrite.
5. **Set up the campaign broad and simple (Hack 5).** Exact settings below.
6. **Pre-flight the scent and tracking (Hack 6).** Pixel firing, page opening matches the ad promise.
7. **Launch, read, decide (Hack 8).** Gut-check day 1 to 2, hold 3 to 4 days to learn, scale the winner,
   clone it to more niches, zombie the starved ones.
8. **Layer retargeting (Hack 7).** Once TOF feeds the funnel, retarget bouncers with a different offer.

## The copy template (validated structure)

This is the structure that worked (a quoted-question hook, then problem, agitate, turn, proof, soft
close). Fill the blanks. Keep it long. No hard CTA.

```
"[The exact question the buyer thinks looking at the creative]"

It's the first question every [TRADE / IDENTITY] asks when [the trigger moment, e.g. the plans get sent through].

[The job in one line: what it is and where.] Normally it's [the dreaded thing], because the old way means [the specific, painful manual process, with one real, slightly funny, true detail].

[Short punchy triplet of the pain: time. quality. waste.]

That's where the money goes on [category].

This one didn't go that way. [The product outcome as plain fact: cut to the exact radius. designed online, machined, engraved, delivered.] [What the crew did, simple and fast.] No [pain a], no [pain b], no [pain c].

[Soft carousel nudge if carousel, e.g. "Swipe to the last frame. That's what it became."]

If [the recurring pain] is the thing that always [blows your program], it doesn't have to be. [Soft mechanism close: you do X, we do Y, you get Z.]
```

**Copy rules.**
- **Hook passes U-U-S-U:** Urgent, Unique, Specific, Useful. Prefer a real number, radius, or job.
- **Identity word in line 2** (Hack 2). That word is what you swap to clone.
- **Specific and true.** One real, human detail beats ten adjectives. Never invent specs or numbers.
- **Product truth.** State only what is real. For Craftons: we cut, we do not bend.
- **No selling.** No "priced online, send us your radius, buy now" pushiness. Soft mechanism close only.
- **Match the page** (Hack 6). The strongest line should also greet them on the landing page.
- **No em dashes, no en dashes.** Full stops, commas, colons, brackets.

## The creative rules

- Real job, clean. The photo is the content.
- Before/after carousel is the strongest native format: process to payoff (or payoff to process).
- Every carousel should include one product-detail beat (the cut edge, the radius, the engraving) so
  the "made to order" point is visible.
- Card headlines BUILD a story, each U-U-S-U, never flat labels ("The formwork." is a label, not a
  headline).
- Sizes: 1:1 (1080x1080) for carousels (same ratio every slide). 4:5 (1080x1350) for single feed.
  9:16 (1080x1920) for stories and reels.
- A real short clip is a ready-made Reel. Use it for the video placement, do not let Meta auto-add
  music to a static (declined, because it reads as an ad and you cannot vet the track).

## The Meta build (exact settings we used, broad and simple)

For a TOF awareness and traffic push that feeds a funnel the BOF already converts:

- **Objective:** Traffic (OUTCOME_TRAFFIC), optimise **Landing Page Views**. (Graduate to a Sales
  objective on a soft event once volume supports it.)
- **Budget:** campaign budget (CBO), start around $100/day, lowest cost. One campaign, one ad set.
- **Audience:** the country only (e.g. Australia), age floor only, **broad**, no interests. Turn
  Advantage+ audience on (advantage_audience 1). Note: with Advantage+ audience you cannot set a max
  age below 65, so set a floor and leave the top open.
- **Placements:** automatic (Advantage+ placements).
- **Ads:** all creatives in the one ad set so Meta moves spend to the winner inside one learning phase.
  Destination is the builder page with per-ad UTMs (utm_content = the identity trigger).
- **Do not** set an account-level spend cap that would affect other campaigns. The daily budget is the
  control.

Pre-flight before activating: pixel firing on the destination, Page and Instagram connected, landing
page opening congruent with the ad, creative preview checked. Build everything PAUSED, review the real
Meta preview, then flip to Active.

## Metrics and decisions

- **Day 1 to 2:** gut-check CTR and cost per landing page view. If clearly failing, pause. Otherwise do
  not touch it.
- **Learning phase:** hold 3 to 4 days. Do not edit a live ad mid-learning, it resets.
- **Read:** which identity trigger wins on CTR and cost per result.
- **Scale:** raise the campaign budget in steps of 30 percent or less every 2 to 3 days while net cash
  holds. Bigger jumps reset learning.
- **Clone and refresh:** clone the winner to more trades and job types. Ship fresh statics weekly.
- **Score:** net cash, always. Not ROAS percentage, not Meta's Opportunity Score.

## Anti-patterns (mistakes to avoid, learned the hard way)

- Ad-looking catalogue cards with brand chrome. Dead on arrival (Hack 4).
- Taglines slapped over the photo, and a hard CTA in the caption. That is selling, not funnelling.
- Short punchy copy. It starves the algorithm (Hack 5). Go long.
- Generic headlines ("Curves Cut Perfectly"). Fail U-U-S-U. Be specific.
- Repeating one card headline on every carousel slide, or repeating a word across the set. Make each
  one earn its place.
- AI imagery when a real job photo exists. Real leads.
- Inventing technical specs or numbers to sound specific. Only claim what is true.
- Chasing Meta's Opportunity Score or a ROAS percentage instead of net cash.
- Em dashes and en dashes. Never.

## Worked example: Radius Pro TOF (2026-07-22)

- **Asset:** a real concreter job (Ardreagh), curved raised garden beds for an apartment courtyard.
  Two formwork angles plus a 4K finished clip, turned into a before/after 1:1 carousel.
- **Copy:** the question-hook long-form template. Hook "How would you even cut curves like these?",
  identity line "every concreter asks", the Pythagoras-and-jigsaw old way, the turn, proof (swipe to the
  finished courtyard), soft close "punch in your radius, we cut it".
- **Clone:** same ad, one word swapped to "landscaper" (Hack 2).
- **Card headlines that build:** "Curves cut to the exact radius." then "Designed online. Machined.
  Delivered." then "Every curve, exactly to plan."
- **Build:** Traffic, Landing Page Views, $100/day AUD CBO, broad AU, both carousels to radius-online.
- **Record:** `campaigns/meta/radius-pro-tof/LAUNCHED.md` (live IDs and settings).
- **Next:** read the winner at day 3 to 4, clone to Formworkers, and shoot AD4 (curved wall plates) and
  AD5 (curved bench seat) per `campaigns/meta/radius-pro-tof/PHOTO-BRIEF.md`.
