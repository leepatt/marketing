## 3. This strengthens his credibility — genuinely

Three things here are better evidence than the Plot Joinery claim:

1. **He grew his own account from zero to 11K.** That's a demonstrated content skill, on an
   account where he controlled every variable. In some ways it's *stronger* evidence than
   Plot, because there's no founder-charisma confound — it's just him.
2. **He built and monetised a 1,800-member community.** That's real operator ability, not just
   content ability. Most people who can make reels cannot do this.
3. **He runs his own paid acquisition.** He understands Meta ads well enough to buy traffic
   into a funnel — even though the ContentOS proposal contains no paid component at all.

So: the man can do this. That was an open question after the first pass. It's less open now.

## 4. But it reframes the offer — and this is the useful part

**Jade's background is info-product and community building.** Courses, a Skool community, paid
ads into a funnel. ContentOS is the next product in that line, and Craftons would be an early
customer of it.

With that in view, re-read the proposal's commercial structure:

| Element in the proposal | What it is |
|---|---|
| "The services I provide for Plot are an investment of **$12,000/mo**" | **Price anchor** |
| "This is also the level of investment I will be asking… in the future" | **Anchor reinforcement** |
| "This programme will start at **$7,000/mo**" | **Intro discount against the anchor** |
| "my priority right now is to bring in the right people… (selfishly, to get great case studies)" | **Discount justification** |
| "**Limited to 3 spots** to ensure quality of delivery" | **Scarcity** |
| 3-month commitment | **Lock-in** |

That is a textbook high-ticket coaching launch — every element, in the standard order. It is
executed well, by someone who demonstrably knows how to run this playbook.

**The implication for Lee:** the $7,000 and $12,000 are **positioning numbers, not cost-based
numbers.** They were chosen to anchor and convert, not derived from what delivery costs him.
Nothing improper about that — it's competent marketing. But it means **the price is soft**, and
the counter-offer play (pay for the strategy and workflow build as a one-off, skip the
retainer) is far more likely to land than the proposal's framing implies.

## 5. The concern this sharpens

**Transferability — and it's now a sharper worry than after the first pass.**

What Jade has proven, twice, is that he can grow **visually spectacular, broad-appeal content**
to a **consumer/hobbyist audience**:

- Concert photography → aspiring photographers. Huge inherent visual appeal, enormous
  addressable audience, a hobby people actively want to get better at.
- Architectural joinery → architects, builders, design-minded homeowners. Beautiful finished
  rooms, broad aesthetic appeal.

Craftons is **neither**. It's B2B trade, a narrow audience (builders, concreters, chippies), a
technical subject, and an infrequent unplanned purchase. The two proven results share a
characteristic that Craftons does not have.

That doesn't mean he'd fail. It does mean **there is no evidence yet that this skill transfers
to a narrow technical B2B niche** — and Craftons would be the experiment, at $7k/month rising
to $12k.

**Second concern, sharpened:** the moonlighting picture is bigger than first assessed. It isn't
"full-time job + 3 clients". It's **full-time job at Plot + an active info-product business
(community, courses, paid ads) + 3 ContentOS clients**. That matters specifically because what
you'd be buying is weekly 1:1 time and 24/7 access.
