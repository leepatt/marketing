**How architects frame it differently from tradies (their words):**
- **Tolerance, not effort** — "virtually no tolerance in a curved wall." The fear is a visibly-imperfect *result*, not the labour.
- **"Non-standard = risk"** — curves get filed under "anything not standard will be more expensive."
- **The problem is the *supplier*, not the geometry** — cost/feasibility pinned on "the builder's willingness and expertise" / "the right tradespeople." ← **Craftons' opening.**
- **Radius as the cost dial** — "as long as the radius is not too tight…" There's a threshold radius below which cost falls off a cliff. Publishing clear achievable-radius specs answers their central uncertainty.
- **Dominant emotion = risk-aversion about the outcome** ("proceed with caution," "done badly they look clunky"). Certainty — known supplier, known radius, known finish — is the thing they wish existed.
