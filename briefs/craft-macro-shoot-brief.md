# Craft Macro — shoot brief for Tia

_The "Craft Macro" format: extreme close-up, slow-motion footage of material doing its thing,
shot beautifully, sound on, that sells nothing. Pure scroll-stop. The Craftons "cheese-pull."
See `CONTENT-PILLARS.md` (Entertain-via-beauty) and `inspiration/SWIPE-FILE.md` (Gozney/Bellroy/July)._

## Non-negotiable rules (every clip)
- **Studio-clean, NEVER a workshop tour.** Plain backdrop (matte black or warm off-white), no clutter, no mess, no shed in frame. We look like a big national company, not a small workshop.
- **Sound on.** Capture the real sound (the cut, the creak, the click, the pour). Sound design is the cheapest premium upgrade — record it clean.
- **Slow-mo: shoot 60fps minimum** (120fps for the fast moments — the cut, the snap, the pour) so it slows smoothly.
- **Vertical 9:16**, framed to crop clean for Reels.
- **One thing per clip.** Don't cram. Each shot = a single moment.
- **5–15 seconds, designed to loop.**
- **Sell nothing.** No captions explaining a product, no CTA. The beauty is the whole point.
- **Light it.** Hard raking light shows edges, curves and texture — that's what makes raw material look premium.

## Session A — Studio (do anytime, banks the reliable baseline)
Plain backdrop, controlled light. These don't need a job site.

### Shot 3 — Flat sheet → curve flex
- **What:** a sheet of bending ply flexing into a radius, in-hand or on a former.
- **Framing:** tight on the bend as the curve forms; let the sheet fill the frame.
- **Move:** slow, steady flex — no jerk.
- **Sound:** the woody creak of the flex.
- **Why:** "it actually bends" — satisfying, and keeps the product legible.

### Shot 5 — The fit / seam
- **What:** two precision parts seating together (formwork pieces locking, or a clean joint closing).
- **Framing:** macro on the seam as the gap disappears to a perfect line.
- **Move:** slow push together; hold on the closed seam for a beat.
- **Sound:** the click / seat.
- **Why:** says "engineered" without a word.

### Shot 9 — Nested kit-of-parts
- **What:** precision-cut parts nested in the sheet, or stacked in rhythm.
- **Framing:** top-down or raking macro across the repeating pattern.
- **Move:** slow slide/pan along the parts, or a hand placing the last piece.
- **Sound:** soft placing clicks / ambient.
- **Why:** efficient, engineered, zero-waste — proof of precision.

## Session B — Pour day, on site (the hero shots — grab when a real job allows)
Shoot these in chronological order on one pour — together they're also a mini-sequence reel.

### Shot 6 — The pour ⭐
- **What:** concrete flowing into the curved form, filling the shape.
- **Framing:** macro on the concrete hitting/filling the curved formwork edge.
- **Move:** locked-off or slow push-in; 120fps for the flow.
- **Sound:** the glug / splash of the pour.
- **Why:** process drama — the curve being born inside the Craftons product.

### Shot 1 — The form-strip reveal ⭐ (the money shot)
- **What:** peeling the plywood form away to reveal the flawless curved concrete face.
- **Framing:** start tight on the form, follow the peel to expose the smooth concrete curve.
- **Move:** slow reveal; let the concrete "arrive."
- **Sound:** the crack / suction of the form releasing.
- **Why:** product + payoff in a single shot — the "cake reveal."

### Shot 2 — Light across the finished curve
- **What:** macro pan along the smooth off-form concrete, raking light revealing the arc.
- **Framing:** graze the surface so light travels along the curve.
- **Move:** slow, smooth slider/pan.
- **Sound:** ambient / leave clean for a music bed.
- **Why:** pure outcome beauty — makes you want to touch it.
