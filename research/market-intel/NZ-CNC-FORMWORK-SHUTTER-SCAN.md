## 4. The method, as observed elsewhere (teardown)

Nobody in NZ is *marketing* this. But the rib-and-plate approach is real and running overseas —
useful as proof the engineering works, and as a source of detail.

**Sapiens Routing / CNC Cutting Services (Canada)** — closest confirmed match to Craftons' concept.
Sells exactly this as a service: CAD/drawings in, **CNC-routed plywood formwork plates** out, "clean
edges, accurate dimensions, and perfect repeatability." Critically: **every part is labelled with a
unique part number** so crews assemble without drawings. That part-numbering detail is the operational
unlock — it's what converts a pile of ply into something a labourer assembles correctly first time.

**Digital Mould Design (Australia)** — `digitalmoulddesign.com`. Formwork for complex/compound-curve
concrete, Australia-wide. Method is technology-flexible: laser-cut steel, CNC-machined and hot-wire-cut
EPS foam, fibreglass, 3D printing. Mould sections built in **handleable sizes that lock or bolt
together on site** to make runs of any length — the modular-section logic Craftons would need.

**The generic method, as documented across sources:** internal plywood ribs fixed to the inside of the
form to stop it bulging under pressure, conventionally spaced **300–600mm** and cut from 12–16mm ply.
Craftons' 25mm BC at wider spacing is a heavier, stiffer variant of a known approach — the novelty is
not the rib, it's **keying it into a machined plate** so the panel self-jigs instead of being measured
and nailed. The joinery vocabulary for that (trapezoidal wedge tabs, press-fit roots, dog-bones at
internal corners) is well catalogued in CNC panel-joinery references.

**Adjacent tech worth knowing:** PERI runs a CNC cutting + formwork assembly service. Odico
(Denmark) and the "Parametric Adjustable Mould" research line attack the same problem with robotics
and reconfigurable pin-beds. Nobody found is doing 25mm-ply-soldier-into-machined-plate as a
productised system — **which is the interesting finding for Craftons.**

   buying 25mm ply in odd quantities and doing something unusual with it.
