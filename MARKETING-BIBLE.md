# The Craftons Marketing Bible

_The operating doctrine for how we market Craftons and run our ads. Built on the playbook of the
marketer Lee follows (Sabri Suby / King Kong — $7.8B in client sales, AFR #1 agency, the radio-ad
origin story), translated point-by-point into concrete Craftons actions. This is the "why" and the
"how it fits together." The ordered to-do list lives in `MARKETING-CHECKLIST.md`._

> **Read order each session:** `STATUS.md` (what's done / next) → this doc (the doctrine) →
> `CONTENT-PILLARS.md` + `SOCIAL-VOICE.md` (the content engine) → `brand/keyword-plan.md` +
> `campaigns/adwords/` (the paid engine). This bible sits *above* those and ties them together.

---

## 0. The one-line doctrine

**Nobody searching for curved work cares about Craftons. They care about their set-out fitting, their
deadline, and not doing the curve three times. We win by understanding that fear better than they can
say it themselves, then being the obvious, lowest-risk way to make it go away.**

Everything below is machinery for that one sentence.

---

## 0.1 The core mindset shift (the thing most of our competitors never accept)

From the source, brutal truth #1: *no one gives a toss about you, your company, or how hard you've
worked — the market is a weighing scale that only transacts on value.* Big River, Bowens, Bunnings and
every generic ply supplier market **inside-out**: "our product, our range, get a quote." The whole
Craftons content doctrine already rejects this (value-first, brand barely there — `CONTENT-PILLARS.md`).
This bible extends that same rule to **the offer and the ads**, which is the one place we currently
still market inside-out ("get a quote").

Two tones, one strategy — do not confuse them (this is already a logged learning in `STATUS.md`):

- **Social / top-of-funnel = value-first, soft-CTA, brand barely there.** Earn the scroll. Never hard-sell.
- **Offer + paid ads = direct response.** Lead with the outcome and the risk reversal, capture the lead,
  nurture. Harder CTA — but the *ad still isn't about us*, it's about their problem.

Sabri's playbook does **not** contradict our value-first law. It is the same law, applied further down
the funnel. Where he's more aggressive than us today is in two specific places we're currently weak:
**(a) a real risk-reversal offer**, and **(b) capturing the 97% who aren't ready to buy today.** Those
two gaps are the reason for this document.

## 1. PILLAR ONE — Own the market's mind before you write a word

> *"It's not how you say things, it's what you say. Spend 80% of your time researching the market. The
> best marketers don't guess what customers want — they camp out inside the mind of their dream prospect."*

### 1.1 Why this is our biggest edge

We already have the receipts that this works: **"bendy ply"** and **"curved bench seat"** are confirmed
job-winners (`keyword-plan.md`) — not because they're clever, but because they're the *exact words the
market already uses*. That is Sabri's whole point proven on our own account. The job now is to make that
systematic instead of accidental.

### 1.2 Who we're actually talking to — the dream buyer

Not "builders." One person (from `brand/audience.md`):

> **A carpenter / formworker / shopfitter with a curved job on the board and a deadline.** Curved work
> is the part of the job he's least sure about. He's done it the slow way before — kerf-cutting,
> laminating sheets, bog-and-sand — and it was slow, wasteful, and cracked at the join. His real fear
> isn't price; it's **the curve not fitting on site and blowing the program in front of the builder.**

Architects/specifiers are the secondary buyer (they spec it in; their fear is "can this curve actually
be built to budget"). We architect everything around the primary buyer — the secondary one comes along
for free, exactly as Sabri says.

**The 20% rule (from the source):** find the 20% of customers who drive 80% of profit and build the
whole funnel around them. → **Checklist item:** pull Shopify + quote data and identify who our best
jobs actually come from (repeat trade buyers? formwork? a particular job size?). We're currently
guessing; the data exists.

### 1.3 The market-intelligence system (build this — it's the highest-ROI work we can do)

Sabri: *"a continuous, living, breathing document — mine the data, use their language verbatim, never
put it in your own words."* We build a standing intel system, not a one-off:

**Where to camp out (the sewers):**
- **Reddit** — r/AusConstruction, r/carpentry, r/Formwork1, r/woodworking (curved/bending threads).
- **Facebook trade groups** — AU carpentry / formwork / shopfitting groups.
- **Our own goldmine** — the quote form submissions (443 lead-form conversions already tracked),
  call notes, the configurator sessions, and email replies. This is first-party intel our competitors
  can't see. **Instrument the quote form to capture richer data** (what are they building, deadline,
  what they've tried) — checklist item.
- **Comments** — on our own curved-work posts and on competitors' / benchmark accounts.
- **Reviews** — our reviews and competitors' one-stars (their one-stars are our positioning).

**The verbatim rule (do not break this):** we lift the market's phrasing raw into a swipe doc and use it
as-is in headlines, ad copy, lead-ins and email. We do **not** paraphrase it into "brand voice." (Our
brand voice already sounds like a builder — `voice-profile.md` — so this is a natural fit, not a
conflict. "bendy ply" beats "flexible plywood substrate" every time.)

**Where it lives:** a living `research/market-intel/` file (verbatim quotes, sorted by pain/desire/
objection/language), refreshed continuously and mined with AI. This becomes the raw material for every
ad, page and email we write. **All the money is made in the research.**

### 1.4 The one hard rule of Pillar One

> Before any ad, page, or email is written, the brief must quote the market's *own words* for the
> problem it addresses. No verbatim quote = not ready to write. We articulate their problem better than
> they can, so it reads like we hacked their Gmail.

## 2. PILLAR TWO — Fish the whole pyramid, not just the 3%

> *"3% are ready to buy now — everyone fights over them, it's a bloodbath. The real money is the 97%
> who have the problem but aren't buying today. Lead with value, capture them, control the market."*

### 2.1 This is our single biggest strategic gap right now

Look at what we run today:
- **AdWords** → "curved plywood / curved formwork / get a quote" = **fishing the 3%** (bottom of funnel,
  high intent, high competition). Correct to run, but it's the red ocean.
- **Value-first social** → beautiful, builds brand, earns attention across the whole pyramid — but has
  **no lead-capture bridge.** A builder who's 3 months from a curved job watches our reel, thinks "nice,"
  and scrolls on. We never got his details. We can't nurture him. When he's finally ready, he Googles
  "curved plywood" and lands in the 3% auction with everyone else.

**The missing middle is the whole game.** We have brilliant top-of-funnel (social) and bottom-of-funnel
(AdWords quote) and **nothing to connect them.** Sabri's fortune was built precisely in that gap.

### 2.2 The Craftons pyramid

| Tier | Share | State of mind | What they search / do | How we serve them |
|------|-------|---------------|-----------------------|-------------------|
| **Buy now** | 3% | Curved job on the board *today* | "curved plywood cut to size", "curved formwork" → get a quote | AdWords → configurator / quote (**already built**) |
| **Info-gathering** | 17% | Job coming, working out how | "how to set out a curved wall", "min bending radius" | How-To content + **lead magnet capture** (gap) |
| **Problem-aware** | 20% | Knows curves are a pain, not looking yet | reads/watches curved-work content | Value-first social + retargeting (gap) |
| **Not problem-aware** | 60% | Doesn't know there's a better way than bog-and-sand | scrolling | Craft Macro / scroll-stop reach (**already strong**) |

Our job as marketers: **move each tier up the pyramid** with content and nurture — not just harvest the
top 3%.

### 2.3 The two-offer structure that unlocks the 97%

Sabri's move: replace the *hard offer* ("get a quote / talk to sales") with a *soft, high-value offer*
that's less threatening, so you capture the info-gatherers **and** win a bigger share of the ready-buyers
(his offers converted 300–500% higher because "download this" beats "get a quote").

Craftons runs **two** offers:

1. **The soft offer we already own (under-marketed): the online builders.** "Design your curved bench
   seat / architrave and get instant pricing — no sales call, no waiting." This is *literally* Sabri's
   less-threatening offer (instant answer vs. "get a quote and speak to a salesperson"). We have it built
   and we barely lead with it. **Fix: make "get instant pricing online" the headline offer in ads and on
   pages, not "get a quote."**
2. **The lead magnet we need to build (captures the pure 97%): a high-value content offer.** A genuinely
   useful, instant-download asset that trades hard value for a name/email/phone — e.g. **"The Curved
   Work Field Guide"**: minimum bending radii by thickness, how to set out a curved wall without cracks,
   bending ply vs kerf vs laminate, how to spec off-form that passes. It maps 1:1 onto our validated
   keyword pillars (`keyword-plan.md`), so the traffic already exists. Capture → nurture by email →
   convert to a configurator quote when the job lands.

### 2.4 The three oceans (where we choose to compete)

- **Red ocean** ("curved plywood Melbourne", "get a quote") — 99% compete here; price-driven; we run it
  for the 3% but we do **not** try to win the war here.
- **Orange ocean** (buyers wanting *proof* it works) — served by **Built with Craftons** (real jobs,
  credited) + reviews + case studies. More skill, bigger market.
- **Blue ocean** (uncontested, value-driven) — served by teaching the whole craft: How-To Series, the
  Field Guide, "how this curve was built." Requires storytelling and education; nobody in AU curved-work
  owns it. **This is where we build the moat.** (Ties directly to brutal truth #8: *the fishing is best
  where the fewest go* — same instinct that made the King Kong radio ad work.)

## 3. PILLAR THREE — Give the market crack (win the attention war)

> *"You're not competing with competitors — you're competing with everything more entertaining on the
> feed. Consumption precedes conversion. Make your ads not look like ads."*

Our content doctrine already lives here (Craft Macro = the scroll-stop, `CONTENT-PILLARS.md`), so Pillar
Three is mostly **codifying rules we half-follow and pointing them at the ads too.**

### 3.1 The attention rules (apply to every ad and every post)

1. **Make ads not look like ads.** Native content, not a catalogue card. If it reads as an ad, it's dead
   on arrival. (Our value-first law already says this — now enforce it in *paid* creative.)
2. **Talk to one human in their language.** Builder-to-builder, verbatim market words, feels hand-written.
   (= `voice-profile.md` + Pillar One's verbatim rule.)
3. **Do the opposite of the category.** Swipe-file every competitor (Big River, Bowens, generic ply
   suppliers, formwork brands) and deliberately do the reverse. Their sites are spec sheets and "request
   a quote"; ours are gorgeous curves, real jobs, and teaching. **Effort is the moat** — the boring stuff
   is easy, which is exactly why nobody stands out doing it.
4. **The headline gets disproportionate effort.** It's seen more than anything else; wrong headline =
   dead on arrival regardless of everything else. Steal *structure* from the highest-traffic headline
   writers on earth (news, tabloids). Every headline must pass **U-U-S-U**:
   - **Urgent** — why look now?
   - **Unique** — not the thousandth "curved plywood supplier" line.
   - **Specific** — a number, a radius, an exact job. ("Bend 12mm ply to a 340mm radius without it
     cracking" beats "flexible plywood available".)
   - **Useful** — their life is better for reading it.
5. **The offer must exist visibly.** Don't bury it. Lead with the outcome.

### 3.2 The one caveat for Craftons

The source's "ride an elephant / be topless / be outrageous" attention tactics do **not** override our
**"big company, never small workshop" law** (`CONTENT-PILLARS.md`). Our version of pattern-disrupting
is **precision and beauty**, not stunts: the form-strip reveal, the flawless off-form curve, the number
on the radius. We disrupt the scroll with *craft*, not clowning. Restraint = looking big — that's our
polarising move in a category of ugly spec sheets.

## 4. THE OFFER — our Godfather Offer (brutal truths #4 & #5)

> *"The money isn't in your thing, it's in the selling of your thing. If your offer doesn't keep you up
> at night, it isn't strong enough. Specific outcome + a number + a hero mechanism (category of one) +
> risk reversal (you carry the risk)."*

Right now Craftons has products and a configurator but **not an offer** in Sabri's sense. Here's the one
we build everything around:

### 4.1 The Craftons Godfather Offer (draft — for Lee to approve/sharpen)

> **"Send us your set-out. We'll CNC-cut your curved parts, label every piece, and deliver them ready to
> fix. If a single part doesn't seat to your line, we re-cut it free and cover the freight."**

Deconstructed against the source's formula:
- **Specific outcome:** curved parts that turn up cut, labelled, ready to fix — no bog-and-sand, no
  re-doing the curve. (Straight from the dream buyer's #1 want in `audience.md`.)
- **The number / proof:** cut on our CNC to *your* set-out — precision no site method matches.
- **Hero mechanism (category of one):** *design it online → instant price → delivered as a labelled,
  ready-to-assemble kit.* Nobody else in AU curved-work offers the online builder **and** a fit
  guarantee. This is what stops us being compared line-for-line with generic ply suppliers.
- **Risk reversal:** *fits first time or we re-cut it free + cover freight.* This kills the buyer's exact
  nightmare — the curve not fitting on site and blowing the program. When the risk is on us, his internal
  question flips from "what if it doesn't fit?" to "how are they confident enough to guarantee that?" —
  and that's when CAC drops.

If the guarantee doesn't make us slightly nervous about delivering it, it isn't strong enough. That
nervousness is the point — it forces our operations to be genuinely tight, and it's uncopyable by anyone
who isn't actually precise.

### 4.2 Offer discipline

- The offer is **one clear promise**, not a feature list. "Instant pricing, endless shapes, curved
  bench seats, architraves, formply…" is a catalogue, not an offer.
- Every landing page, ad and email carries the **same** offer + risk reversal. Consistency is what makes
  it land.
- **Feasibility gate:** the fit guarantee has commercial and ops implications (tolerances, what counts as
  "our fault" vs. a bad set-out from the customer, freight cost exposure). This is a **Lee decision** —
  the checklist flags it as a gate before we put it in market.

- **The Godfather offer + configurator converts** at the bottom.

Retargeting stitches it together (anyone who watched/read but didn't convert → gets the soft offer again).

## 6. THE SCOREBOARD — what we optimise for (brutal truth #6)

> *"F*** ROAS. You can't pay overhead with ROAS. The only thing that matters is net cash at the end of
> the month — how many money-soldiers come back to camp."*

**Our north-star metric: net profit per month from marketing — not ROAS, not CPL, not CPC.**

Concretely for Craftons:
- We already have solid conversion tracking (`campaigns/adwords/conversion-tracking.md` — 23 purchases /
  $17.3k + 443 lead forms tracked). **Do not launch anything without tracking** (logged learning — CNC
  Cut burned ~$2k/mo blind).
- Track the **full funnel economics**, not vanity metrics: cost per lead → lead-to-quote rate →
  quote-to-job rate → **average job value & margin** → **CAC vs. lifetime value** (trade buyers repeat —
  LTV is the whole argument for spending up).
- **Don't cap spend on an arbitrary CPL/CPA.** If a lead costs more but the job margin and repeat value
  justify it, scale it. The constraint people put on themselves ("I won't go over $X per lead") is
  self-imposed. Decide how big we want Craftons to be, then allocate to get there.
- **Demote vanity conversions:** `file_download` should be Secondary, not Primary (logged in `STATUS.md`)
  — don't optimise toward PDF-grabbers. (Note the nuance: the Field Guide lead magnet *is* a download,
  but we optimise toward the **lead + eventual job**, not the download count itself.)

## 7. THE OPERATING LAWS (the rest of the brutal truths)

Marketing sits inside a business. These are the source's business laws, kept short, as guardrails:

- **#2 — 80% mindset.** This is a deliberate long build (the standing rule: *build deliberately, don't
  rush, lock each piece*). Delay gratification; reinvest wins into the engine.
- **#3 — Without a team/system you have a job, not a business.** For us this means **systemise the
  marketing** so it isn't Lee-dependent: SOPs, the intel system, named repeatable content formats
  (already the doctrine), and the engine (this repo) running the recurring work.
- **#7 — Businesses die of indigestion, not starvation.** When we find a channel/offer that spits cash,
  we **ride it** — we don't chase every shiny new tactic (the "Andromeda Facebook struct" trap). Focus
  beats novelty. One working funnel, cranked, beats five half-built ones.
- **#8 — The fishing is best where the fewest go.** Our blue-ocean bet: own AU curved/off-form teaching
  and the online-builder category while competitors sell spec sheets. (The King Kong radio-ad instinct.)
- **#9 — Make it so simple a chimpanzee could run it.** Master the 20% of inputs that drive 80% of
  results, then turn them into checklists (→ `MARKETING-CHECKLIST.md` is the start of this).
- **#10 — No one's opinion pays the bills except the customer's.** Optimise for what buyers do (jobs
  won, cash), not competitor noise or a one-star. Read our five-star results; ignore the stray dogs.
- **#11 — The hard road is the right road.** Building the intel system, the lead magnet, the nurture
  sequence and a real guarantee is harder than boosting a post — which is exactly why it's a moat. At
  every fork, take the road competitors won't.
