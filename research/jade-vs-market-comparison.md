## 1. The real price tag

The proposal reads as "$7,000". The committed and trajectory numbers are larger:

| Horizon | Ex GST | Inc GST |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly (intro) | $7,000 | $7,700 |
| **3-month commitment (the actual decision)** | **$21,000** | **$23,100** |
| Monthly from month 4 | $12,000 | $13,200 |
| **12 months if it continues** (3 × $7k + 9 × $12k) | **$129,000** | **$141,900** |

The $7,000 is an introductory rate on a **$129k/year** path. Worth deciding against the year-one number, not the intro number.

## 2. Cost per deliverable — the swing factor

Nothing in the proposal commits to a volume. Jade edits whatever Craftons films. So the unit cost is set entirely by **how much Jake actually films** — the thing Lee called the million dollar question.

At **$7,000/mo ex GST**:

| Scenario | Posts/mo | Cost per post |
|---|---|---|
| Plot's stated volume (12–20/wk) | 52–87 | **$80 – $135** |
| Strong founder-led output | 20 | $350 |
| Realistic for a flat-out owner new to camera | 8–12 | **$583 – $875** |
| Slow start / production gets busy | 4 | **$1,750** |

At **$12,000/mo** (month 4 onward) every figure above roughly **doubles**.

The offer is excellent value at high volume and poor value at low volume. It transfers all the volume risk to Craftons, because Craftons is the one filming.

**Where Jade lands:**
- At **$7,000** he sits at the **top of the full-service agency band (G)** — while being the only option in the table that **doesn't include filming** and doesn't commit to a volume or touch paid ads.
- At **$12,000** he costs the **same as a full-time in-house employee (J)** — who would film, be there five days a week, and work only for Craftons.

**The fair caveat:** H/I aren't really the same product as F/G. Agencies rent you a service; Jade is selling **capability transfer + high-touch coaching + unlimited editing**. Nobody in the $2–5k band gives a founder a weekly 1:1 and 24/7 text access. That's a real difference and it's genuinely hard to buy — it just isn't what the agency comparison prices.

## 5. What's genuinely strong about the offer

Stated plainly, because the price analysis above is unflattering and the offer has real merit:

1. **High-touch access is rare.** Weekly 1:1s plus 24/7 text from the person who actually did the work. Agencies at $3–5k give you an account manager and a monthly report.
2. **Adjacent, verifiable-ish case study.** Melbourne, trade, selling to builders and architects. That's a much closer match to Craftons than a generic social agency — and this niche is genuinely underserved.
3. **Unlimited editing is uncapped upside.** If Jake gets to even 20 posts/mo, the unit economics beat every other option on the ladder.
4. **Capability transfer, not dependency.** The stated goal is that Craftons owns the workflow after 90 days. Agencies are structured so you never leave.
5. **Removes the hardest bottleneck.** "Never touch a timeline again" is the real blocker for owner-operators. Editing is where founder content dies.
6. **Not a competitor.** Plot does architectural joinery; Craftons does curved building products. Adjacent audience, no direct conflict.

## 6. Structural risks specific to this deal

1. **He's moonlighting.** Solo, still full-time at Plot. Craftons would be buying his evenings and weekends. Three spots at $7k = $21k/mo of side income on top of a full-time job. Worth asking directly how many hours a week Craftons actually gets.
2. **Key-person risk, no backup.** No team. Illness, holiday, or his job getting busy all land on Craftons with no redundancy.
3. **His employer is also his biggest client, at a higher rate.** Plot pays $12k; Craftons $7k. If attention is ever contested, the ranking is not in Craftons' favour.
4. **Craftons carries the filming burden, and that's the untested part.** Jake is new to camera and the workshop is at capacity. Every other option on the ladder either includes filming or costs a fraction of this.
5. **The Plot result has confounds.** Charley Li was already Instagram-forward and has hosted the *Site to Studio* podcast since ~2020 — the content engine predates the 12-month window. Plot's product is visually spectacular and the founder is a natural on camera. How much is the system versus Charley plus the product is the open question.
6. **"Content-attributed quotes" is not revenue.** Quotes issued ≠ jobs won ≠ margin. The attribution method was never stated.
7. **The 140K+ is unreconciled.** Public counts total ~81K (IG ~34K, FB ~39K, TikTok ~8K, YouTube unverified). May legitimately include Charley's personal account, the podcast, or be cross-platform reach. Ask for a dated per-platform screenshot, start vs now.
8. **Phase 1 largely duplicates existing work.** `CONTENT-PILLARS.md`, `SOCIAL-VOICE.md`, `brand/`, `SWIPE-FILE.md` and Drive's `MARKETING-BIBLE.md` already lock pillars, voice, audience and angles. A month of "Plan" may be re-buying something Craftons owns.
9. **Capacity contradiction.** CNC Cut is currently declining work for lack of capacity. Craftons jobs are the wanted growth — but the constraint is worth naming out loud before spending to create more demand.
10. **Budget concentration.** This becomes more than all other marketing spend combined, on organic content, with no paid-ads component, at a business that already has a built-but-unlaunched AdWords campaign.
