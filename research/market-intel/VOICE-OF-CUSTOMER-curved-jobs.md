## The questions customers ask (compiled — this is the FAQ + objection list)

1. **"How much / ball-park / what's the l/m rate?"** — price, almost always. Often want a lineal-metre rate.
2. **"Is this achievable / can it still be CNC cut from CAD?"** — can-you-even-do-it uncertainty.
3. **"What's the turnaround / can it be ready by [Friday / date]?"** — lead time, often urgent.
4. **"What ply / thickness would you use for a good finish?"** — they want Craftons' expert recommendation.
5. **"Can you match this profile/design?"** — fidelity to their drawing.
6. **"Can you ship to [interstate]?"** — freight (interstate only).
7. **"Can I send CAD/plans / can you work from this?"** — file handoff.

## Nuggets & patterns

1. **The core product language = "top and bottom plates," "lineal metres / l/m rate," "radius in mm," "tails
   cut to suit on site," "face ply," "bendy ply," "direct stick," "set-out."** Use these verbatim in copy.
2. **They want Craftons to be the expert.** Repeated asks for ply-type/thickness recommendations "to achieve
   a good finish" (Top Form, Policrete). → An **achievable-radius + which-ply-for-what-finish guide** is
   both a value-add and a conversion tool. This is the content builders/concreters actually want.
3. **The #1 friction is files/plans.** "Screenshots won't work — got a PDF or DXF?"; "highlight the plates,
   send only relevant pages." Customers send messy screenshots / full drawing sets. → Make file handoff
   dead-easy (accept a photo/marked-up plan; we extract the set-out). A real conversion lever.
4. **Phone-first, then email.** Nearly every thread: "spoke on the phone," "give me a call," "nice chatting."
   → These jobs convert by *conversation*, not a self-serve online checkout. Tension with an "instant online
   price" offer: the online builder is the *soft entry*; a human closes. Don't remove the phone.
5. **Who buys:** overwhelmingly **concreters/formworkers** (curved formwork plates, curved seats) and
   **builders** (curved wall plates); plus joiners (architraves). Concreters often source **on behalf of a
   builder/landscaper** (Concrete Formed Design ⟶ LBA; Concrete Architecture ⟶ a landscaper).
6. **Why quotes stall (corrected):** most stalled quotes are **local** concreters (Formworx-Werribee,
   Policrete-Brighton, Concretum-Moorabbin) — **not** a freight problem. The driver is **DIY-vs-buy + price +
   speed**: these concreters *can* make the formwork themselves (Policrete: "previously we've used 2×6mm"),
   so Craftons has to beat their in-house method on price, finish, or speed. (Freight *is* a friction for the
   two interstate stalls — Top Form Sydney, Mumford SA.)
7. **Speed decides jobs.** "Price this asap… ready Friday?" Slow quoting/turnaround loses the job.
8. **The brand bridge already exists:** the CNC Cut email signature cross-sells *"Need Custom Radius & Curves?
   — Try Craftons Radius Pro"* → craftons.com.au/products/craftons-radius-pro-formply.
9. **Quotes go out via Quotient** (go.quotientapp.com) — $ values live there; ClickUp holds material, lineal
   metres, deadline, follow-up status.

## Implications (what to do with this)

- **Offer:** the Godfather offer must beat the concreter's *in-house* formwork method — lead with **price
  certainty + speed + guaranteed finish/fit**, not just "we can cut it." Answer "what ply for a good finish"
  *in the offer.*
- **Copy:** write in their words — "top & bottom plates," "l/m rate," "cut to your set-out, tails to suit on
  site," "your radius, any ply, a perfect finish."
- **Content:** the **which-ply / achievable-radius / finish guide** is the highest-value asset (they keep
  asking for it) — doubles as the lead magnet.
- **Conversion ops:** kill the file-handoff friction and quote **fast**; keep the phone in the loop.
- **Product:** curved architraves is tiny but real (De Ree) — decide grow vs park.
