# Script 01 — Formwork Builder soft launch

**On camera:** Lee · **Location:** Fairfield workshop · **Length:** ~45s
**Status:** draft for Lee. Numbers marked `[FILL]` must come off a real job before this shoots.

---

## The structure

| # | Beat | Frame | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demand | Face | Earns the right to talk about the tool |
| 2 | The ask | Face | The customer's words, not ours |
| 3 | The answer | Screen | First look at the app |
| 4 | The moment | Screen, tight | The one detail that makes it click |
| 5 | Payoff | The conveyor | Nest on screen → same nest on the belt, top-down → one plate off |
| 6 | Soft close | Face | Soft, no pitch |

**The payoff is not on site.** It's the nest on screen cutting to the same nest travelling
on the conveyor, shot top-down, then one plate picked off the belt. The nest *is* what the
software worked out, so the match proves the tool did something rather than illustrating it.
No forklift, no site access, and it's footage of something that was going to happen anyway.

---

## The words

> Say it, don't read it. These are the beats in your own mouth, not a teleprompter.
> Where a line feels stiff, change the words and keep the job the line is doing.

**0.0 – 3.5s · FACE · the hook**

> "Concreters kept ringing us for something we didn't make."

*Cold open on your face, mid-sentence energy. No "hey guys", no intro. The first 1.7 seconds
decide the reach of the whole thing, and a question that isn't answered yet is what holds it.*

**3.5 – 11s · FACE · the demand**

> "We launched the Radius Pro for curved walls. Then the calls started coming from
> concreters. Curved seats, curved retaining walls, hearths, firepits."

**11 – 19s · FACE · the ask**

> "And it was the same ask every time. Top and bottom plates, face ply, cut to the set-out,
> bundled up and sent to site. They'd send a drawing and wait days for a price."

*This is the strongest thing in the script because it's verbatim from real enquiries. Keep
the trade words exactly as they are: top and bottom plates, face ply, set-out.*

**19 – 27s · SCREEN · the answer**

> "So we built this. Pick your shape, put your inner radius in."

*The **Wall Shape** row — `Circle · U-Shape · L-Shape · Straight`. Click one, the model
appears in the viewer. Legible in half a second, no caption needed. Hide the browser chrome.*

**27 – 35s · SCREEN, TIGHT · the moment**

> "It gives you your arc and your chord as you go. Then it splits it into plates that fit a
> sheet, and each one comes engraved with its own ID."

*Tight on the live readout under Dimensions — `3770mm arc · 2400mm chord (at the inner
radius)` — while the Inner Radius changes and the numbers tick over.*

*This is the beat that decides whether a concreter takes the app seriously. Arc and chord are
the two numbers he otherwise works out by hand or chases off a drawing: Concrete Architecture
came to us with "just got the internal radius, it is 1300mm" and nothing else. Record the
whole panel at 1440 wide and crop hard in the edit — don't frame it tight while recording.*

**35 – 42s · MATCH CUT → THE CONVEYOR · the payoff**

> *(no voiceover — let it land)*

*Three shots, tight together:*

1. **The match.** *Last screen frame cuts to the belt.* **The viewer is 3D, not a plan
   view** *— so the exact shape-for-shape match isn't automatic. Either orbit the viewer to
   look straight down (check whether it does that; thirty seconds of clicking) and keep the
   overhead belt shot, or end the screen section on the **Order Summary** parts list and cut
   from that to the belt. Parts on a list, parts on a belt — weaker visual match, cleaner
   idea, doesn't depend on an angle you might not get*
2. *The belt running. 8–10 detabbed curved plates travelling through frame. The movement is
   free — you don't have to do anything to it*
3. **Picking one off the belt.** *Hand in, lifts a plate, belt keeps running underneath.
   Close. This is the best second in the video — the drawing becoming a thing you can hold*

> **Not a dry assembly on the floor, and not the cut.** The assembly was wrong: plates laid
> flat only read as a curve from directly above, and pushing them together is slow to watch.
> The cut is the wrong subject — it shows off the machine, and this video is about the
> software. The belt shows the software's *output*, which is what you're claiming it makes.

**42 – 47s · FACE · soft close**

> "It's on the site. Go and break it."

*Soft. No CTA graphic, no "link in bio" on screen. The invitation to break it is doing more
work than a pitch would — it's a soft launch, and asking for faults is how you earn the
second video.*

*Cut away from your face on the last line to a **wide** of you carrying the bundle out past
the roller door. Wide, not close — a strapped bundle has no detail worth seeing, and the shot
isn't carrying information, it's carrying "this leaves here and goes to a job".*

## Known weak points

- **Beat 4 is the risk.** Everything hangs on whether one screen detail reads as impressive
  in a tight shot. If it doesn't, the video is a tour of an app and it will die. Look at
  shot D before committing to the rest
- **The match cut has to be genuinely tight.** A loose one reads as two unrelated clips and
  the payoff evaporates. Top-down, nest order, screen visible while you frame it
- **The viewer is 3D.** Check whether it orbits to a straight-down view before committing to
  the overhead belt shot. If it doesn't, use the Order Summary route instead of forcing a
  match that won't land
- **The plates are detabbed**, so there's no cutting in this video at all. That's deliberate
  here. Bank a spindle shot anyway when it's easy — it's the right payoff for a *Radius Pro*
  video, just not this one
- **Beat 6 asks for faults.** Be ready for people to find them. That's the trade
