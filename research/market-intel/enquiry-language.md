# The market's own words — from the inbox

_First-party intel mined from `cnc@cnccut.melbourne` on 2026-08-03. ~201 threads matched a
curved-work query over 12 months; this is the language and pattern extract._

**Names are first-name-plus-initial per the brand rules. No contact details, no addresses.**

> This exists because `MARKETING-BIBLE.md` §1.4 says: *"Before any ad, page, or email is written, the
> brief must quote the market's own words for the problem it addresses. No verbatim quote = not ready
> to write."* The first ad batch was written without this. It shouldn't have been.

---

## 🔴 The biggest finding: the pain is getting geometry into a cuttable form

This is not in `brand/audience.md`. It is the most repeated friction in the entire inbox, and it
blocks jobs before price is ever discussed.

> **"Plan view needs to be flattened before we can cut."**
> — Craftons to a builder + architect, on a $3,377 façade job

> **"Our Revit software does not have a single 'developed view', (or a flattened curve view) — we can
> export and provide an IFC 3d model if this helps."**
> — Dean I., architect

> **"Please have the architects send a clean CAD file showing the actual shape of..."**
> — Craftons to a contracts administrator

> **"The Base file is too complex to be machined on a CNC, it would have to be completely stripped
> back to simple shapes."**
> — Craftons to an engineering buyer

> **"Sorry for muck around mate, the architect has given us the green light to free hand the render
> to this wall after seeing the CAD!"**
> — Kellan P., contracts administrator — **a job lost to file friction**

**Why this matters commercially:** curved geometry arrives as a plan view, a Revit model or an IFC —
none of which are cuttable. Somebody has to unwrap it. That work currently happens in email, over
days, sometimes involving the architect. **Plan Scan is the product answer to this**, and it is
already live and producing orders.

**And Lee's own reply is the best line in the inbox:**

> **"We can then snap to the architect's lines, ensuring the curves are perfect."**

That is the promise, in the seller's own words, addressing the buyer's real fear.

## How enquiries actually arrive

**Overwhelmingly: they attach a drawing and ask for a price.** Not "I'd like to design something."

> *"I'm looking to get pieces of 9mm ply to the green highlighted areas cut to the radius profile.
> Can you have a look and get back to me ASAP please? I've attached CAD."* — Fintan K., project manager

> *"See attached below drawings. Just looking for this profile for this seat for the corners i've
> highlighted in yellow. Can you give us a price?"* — Sandrow Y.

> *"I've attached the plan for a new job and attached highlighted screenshots of what we need. Can I
> get a price for..."* — Sinan T.

> *"Can you please provide a quote for the following profile, 17mm formply, Qty 1200."* — David M., senior project engineer

**The recurring shape:** *attach file → highlight the bit they want → ask for a price → ask lead time.*

## The product, in customers' words

**"Top and bottom plates"** is the phrase, and it confirms the order data.

> *"**Top and bottom plates** please mate, the seat only runs to the left hand side of the fireplace,
> the rest is blocked up 👍"* — Matt P., concreter, on a concrete bench seat radius

> *"Can we please get the **tops and bottoms** of the concrete profiles machined out. We will require
> the bottom templates with a 72mm hole."* — Sean C., director

> *"We could supply just the **curved top and bottom plates**?"* — Craftons, offering the product

**Other real job language:** *concrete bench seat radius · formply profiles · packer plate · façade
columns · feature walls · curved 90mm stud walls, double top and double bottom · fireplace render
template · war memorial.*

## Lead time is asked constantly — and loses jobs

> *"Please also confirm lead time."* · *"Do you know approximate lead time once materials..."*
> — Fiachra S., site engineer, asked twice in one thread

> *"Unfortunately, our lead time is currently three to four weeks, so we won't be able to help you out
> with this job."* — Craftons, **declining a job**

⚠️ **Note the tension with the ads.** Order data says 2-day turnaround on Radius Pro; this reply
quotes 3–4 weeks for a general CNC job. **Radius Pro turnaround and CNC-Cut job turnaround are
different things**, and an ad must not blur them. Worth confirming which applies when.

## 🔴 Audience correction — these are commercial construction buyers

`brand/audience.md` says *"carpenters, formworkers, shopfitters, cabinetmakers... on the tools."*
The inbox says otherwise. Roles seen enquiring:

**Site Engineer · Project Manager · Contracts Administrator · Site Manager · Director · Senior
Project Engineer**

At tier-1 and mid-tier contractors, on named projects — ring-road infrastructure, private residential
builds, war memorials, façades. Several enquiries loop in the **architect**.

**Implication for the ads:** the reader may be procuring rather than installing. They care about lead
time, file format, price certainty and programme — not about avoiding bog-and-sand personally. That
does not delete the on-the-tools buyer, but it is not the only one, and the current angles assume it.

## Language to lift verbatim

**They say:** cut to the radius profile · top and bottom plates · tops and bottoms · profile ·
machined out · templates · developed view · flattened · snap to the lines · lead time · lineal metres
· can you give us a price · have a look and get back to me ASAP

**They don't say:** set-out (rare) · bog-and-sand (**never once in this sample**) · kerfing (never) ·
bendy ply (not in enquiries — it's a *search* term, not an email term)

⚠️ **"Stop bog-and-sanding curves on site" — my Angle 2 headline — does not appear anywhere in the
inbox.** It came from `audience.md`, which may reflect an older or different buyer. Worth testing,
but it is not the language this sample uses.

## What this changes

| Current assumption | What the inbox says |
|---|---|
| Buyer is on the tools | Often a PM, site engineer or contracts admin |
| Pain is rework and bog-and-sand | Pain is **getting geometry into a cuttable file**, and lead time |
| They want to design online | They **attach a drawing and ask for a price** |
| "Set-out" is the frame | **"Top and bottom plates"** and **"profile"** are the frame |

### The angle the evidence actually points at

> **"Send us the plan. We'll snap to the architect's lines and cut the plates."**

Every element is evidenced: *send us the plan* (how they buy), *snap to the architect's lines* (Lee's
own promise), *the plates* (their word for the product). And it maps onto the Drive bible's Godfather
Offer, which starts *"Send us your set-out."*
