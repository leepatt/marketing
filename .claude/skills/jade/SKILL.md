# Digital Jade — Craftons content coach

Built from the Jade Ryuki method (26 transcribed reels + two Skool posts) and then **verified against
@plotjoinery itself**: 148 posts across a 90-day window, 170 reels transcribed in full. Where the two
disagree, the Plot Joinery data wins. Translated for Craftons.

**Triggers:** what should we film, script this reel, plan a shoot, is this hook any good, content ideas for
this job, we've got nothing to post, why isn't this working.

**Source evidence:** `01 Inspiration/plotjoinery/` and `01 Inspiration/jade.ryuki/` on Drive (transcripts,
captions.md, digest.json, Skool source docs). Our analysis:
`research/inspiration-style-guides/plotjoinery.md` (the account that performs) and `jade.ryuki.md` (the

brands, which agrees with almost all of this.

**Always pair with `craftons-voice`** for any words that reach a customer. This skill decides what to make.
That one decides how it sounds.

## 1. The operating beliefs

These are the load-bearing ones. Everything else follows.

**Volume comes before judgement.** Plot Joinery was making good weekly videos at a week of effort each.
They raised cadence 5 to 10x and accepted lower polish to do it. The founding moment: the founder filmed
a quick story on his phone, one take, no mic, no lights, and it did about double their average. Seven
days of effort versus one, at double the return.

**The point of volume is not more shots on goal, it is finding the winners faster.** You cannot know
which idea works before you post it. Post enough to find out, then pour effort only into what proved
itself. "We didn't question our strategy until we had at least 100 reels out."

**The hit rate is 1 in 10 to 20, and this is now measured, not claimed.** Across 148 Plot Joinery posts:
a 3x-median post arrives **1 in 8.7**, a 5x-median post **1 in 18.5**. He quoted "once every 10 to 20"
before anyone could check it, and the data matches. At two posts a day, a real outlier lands weekly. Never
read a flat fortnight as failure, and never change strategy on a small sample.

**Cadence, for reference.** Plot Joinery ran 1.17 posts/day in June rising to **2.2/day in September**, and
climbing all quarter. Our benchmark set runs 11 to 30 posts a month. They do twice a day.

**The rule of one, and it is the best-evidenced thing in the whole method.** Their instinct on site was to
cram every detail of a kitchen into one reel. Cutting to one detail per video gave them 10x.

Two Plot Joinery posts, **same day (5 July), same account, same product area, same caption format**:

| Post | Likes |
|---|---|
| "The reason that this drawer doesn't span the entire width…" | **3,000** |
| "Let's have a look at this shaving cabinet and vanity unit…" | **124** |

The winner takes **one detail** and gives the reason for it four times over. The loser is a **tour**:
mirror shaver, two cupboards, adjustable shelf, drawers each end, docking GPOs, doors, outriggers. Seven
features, no reasons. **24x apart, structure the only variable.**

**The tour is the failure mode, and it is exactly what Craftons would produce by instinct** when asked to
make content about a finished job. Ten benefits about one thing beats one benefit about ten things. The
bonus is arithmetic: one job becomes eight videos instead of one.

**Follow your curiosity, then filter through the customer.** The clever technique or the back-end system
you are proud of will pull big numbers from other people in your trade, not from buyers. Interesting to
you is the starting filter, not the finishing one.

**None of it matters if the work is bad.** His line, and worth keeping.

### The Craftons guardrail on "post ugly"

His low-production argument is stage-specific and he says so: he is building a personal brand from zero
and polish is not the constraint. **Craftons is not in that position.** We sell finish quality on premium
building products, and a scrappy reel of a bad-looking part actively costs us.

Resolve it this way: **low production effort, never low subject quality.** One take on a phone is fine.
Shaky, badly lit, or showing a part that is not clean is not. The finished component in shot is the
product shot, always. Cheap to make, never cheap to look at.

## 2. The three content types

The spine of the whole method. Roughly even mix.

**1. Finished work.** Proves we can make what they want. Cheapest to produce because the job already
happened. For Craftons: the arc off the machine, the formwork braced and ready to pour, the stripped wall,
the architrave sitting perfect on first fit.

**2. Process.** Pulls the curtain back on how it gets made. Captures the interesting engineering, and
builds trust by showing the work. **This is the one that needs a person on site at the exact moment**, and
it is the format Craftons is worst positioned for right now. It is also the highest-value.

**3. Systems.** What we do differently to competitors that cuts time, effort or risk for the customer.
**This is Craftons' unfair advantage and nobody else in the trade can copy it.** Configure online, nested
offsite, part IDs on every piece, setout supplied, dispatched in days. Our master synthesis calls this
Pillar 4, the mechanism and the wedge. Jade's clients cannot make this content. We can.

### The four archetypes that actually performed

Measured on @plotjoinery's 90-day window, ranked by what the data supports.

**a. The single-detail explainer — the workhorse.** One detail, one reason it exists, 30 seconds, no intro
or outro. Their best (4,680 likes) is four sentences, three beginning "the reason":

> "The reason that this drawer doesn't span the full width of the cupboard is because we have created this
> little space to accommodate a GPO. And the reason that the GPO is set back is so that when you plug
> something in, it does not hit the back of the drawer…"

A decision explained, not a feature shown. **Craftons can copy this tomorrow with no new capability.**

**b. Problem > Solution > Benefit — the heavy hitter.** See §3. Present verbatim in three of their top five.

**c. Naming the avatar's blind spot.** "Architects love designing HVAC units above joinery, but no one
really thinks about how the filters are going to get changed." The specifier is named in four words and the
hook is a problem they cause. Their most-commented post of the quarter (748 comments) is this case study.

**d. Unscripted jeopardy — biggest single hit, least repeatable.** Their top post of the quarter (6,013
likes) is **not a case study at all**. Raw workshop banter, swearing included, captioned *"(no more material
if we got this wrong)"*. No framework, no script. This matches our master synthesis finding that spikes come
from people and story, not objects, and it is the one thing the frameworks do not explain. We have real
versions of this: a committed first cut, a MASSO retrofit mid-commissioning, a sheet with no spare.

## 2b. The video framework — the actual anatomy

How a Plot Joinery video is built, measured across 148 posts. This is the shape to copy.

### It is a two-tier system

- **Short-form reel = the trailer.** 30 to 60 seconds. One detail, or one case study. Earns the reach.
- **Numbered long-form case study = the destination.** Unlocked by a comment gate (§10). Does the selling.

Almost nobody copies the second tier, which is why almost nobody gets their comment counts.

### Four beats, every time

1. **Hook (0 to 2 seconds): the reason, or the constraint.** No intro, no name, no logo. The first word is
   already the content.
2. **Mechanism, step by step, with real numbers.** They name materials and dimensions constantly: "21 mil
   thick MDF frame", "25 mil by 25 mil equal angle welded into a square frame", "five mil fluted glass",
   "two to three mil door gaps". The specificity *is* the credibility.
3. **Benefit, stated explicitly.** Often literally enumerated: "Okay, what are the actual benefits of this
   solution? First of all… Our second benefit… Third benefit… And finally…"
4. **No outro.** No sign-off, no subscribe, no branding. It stops when the point is made. The caption
   carries the gate.

### Length: their most common length is not their best length

| Spoken length | Posts | Median likes |
|---|---|---|
| under 300 chars | 10 | 135 |
| 300 to 600 | 71 | 166 |
| **600 to 900** | 38 | **275** |
| 900 to 1,400 | 16 | 223 |
| over 1,400 | 3 | 1,512 |

The modal choice (300 to 600 characters, ~25 to 40 seconds) is mediocre. **600 to 900 characters, roughly
45 to 70 seconds of speech, outperforms it by 65%.** Do not truncate to fit an imagined attention span.
Explain the thing properly, then stop.

### The single highest-leverage phrase

Ten posts in the window say **"the reason"** out loud. Their median is **443 likes against 219** for
everything else. Saying *why* rather than *what* is, on this evidence, worth roughly double.

## 3. The script framework: Problem > Solution > Benefit

His highest-performing structure. Credited with 18,000+ follows and 3.7M views across 7 videos, and
8,775 followers off a single post. Use it for any job that had a real constraint.

It is a case study told as a story. He frames it as slaying a dragon.

### Part I — Problem

Open on a hyper-specific problem, in the first two seconds. Usually a hard client brief. His example:

> "The island bench had a massive 900mm overhang. Most island benches have a 300, maybe 400mm overhang.
> This one was really big."

A problem hook does three jobs at once: grabs attention, calls out the ideal customer by speaking about
their world, and opens a loop they have to close.

Then raise the stakes. What happens if it is not solved? What counts as success?

> "We knew that if we didn't solve this problem, the stone could crack, or the island could topple."

**The bigger the dragon, the better the story.** Show the problem, do not just say it. B-roll, a diagram,
an animation. Take the mental load off the viewer.

*Craftons openings, as a shape:* a radius tighter than anything standard will hold. A wall where every
panel is a different arc. A programme with no time for a chippie to cut curves on site. A tolerance that
has to be right first time because the pour is booked.

### Part II — Solution

**Walk the dead ends first.** The approaches you tried or considered that would not work. This is the part
most people skip and it is the part that does the work. It shows expertise (you knew the options),
willingness (you kept going for the customer), and vulnerability (you showed behind the curtain). All
three build trust.

> "A standard MDF substrate wouldn't work. And even adding a simple steel frame, like we normally would
> for a 300 or 400mm overhang, wouldn't fully solve the problem either. We also knew from speaking with
> the customer that the big overhang was non-negotiable."

Then deliver what worked. A sentence or a paragraph, whatever the problem needs.

**The dead ends are Jake's to name, not ours to invent.** Ask him. "What would most people have tried
here, and why would it have gone wrong?" Never fabricate a technical dead end. A builder will spot it
instantly and it costs more credibility than the video earns.

### Part III — Benefit

The "so what". The story is incomplete without it. Did we solve it? Did we hit the criteria set at the
start? Who benefited?

> "We met the design intent: the 900mm overhang. It met our buildability and functionality standards. And
> there was no additional cost, no variation, because we priced it all in during quoting. And no delay on
> site, because it was all planned for."

Note what that closes on: **cost, variation, programme.** A builder's benefit list, not an emotional one.
It is almost word for word what Craftons already sells. Close there every time.

## 4. Hook patterns

All spoken, inside two seconds. **These are the ones that worked on the client account**, with their
measured likes, which matters more than the ones he uses on his own page.

- **Reason-first.** "The reason that this drawer doesn't span the full width of the cupboard is because…" (4,680)
- **Client constraint list.** "So you want metal cabinet doors, but you don't want any of the following." (3,431)
- **Avatar blind spot.** "Architects love designing HVAC units above joinery, but no one really thinks about how the filters are going to get changed." (924)
- **Offer a technique.** "A corner detail you can consider for the junctions of any wall panelling is what we call a bird's mouth joint." (1,374)
- **Preference as opinion.** "We love lowering the bin a little bit so that we can add in an inner drawer above." (1,224)
- **Plain how-we-did-it.** "Here's how we made these vertical finger pulls on these tall cabinet doors." (1,016)

**What is absent, across all 148 posts:** no "welcome back", no name or logo sting, no question hooks, no
trend audio, no brand intro of any kind. The first word is the content.

For Craftons, the number in the hook should be a site number, not a vanity one. Millimetres, minutes,
sheets, days saved, a radius, a tolerance. "A 2.4 metre radius, and not one panel cut on site."

## 5. The ten formats

His list, straight out of DcvnCd_zRIl. Use it when the mix has gone stale.

1. Show what you built
2. Talk about how it was built
3. Show the process of it being built
4. Answer FAQs
5. Answer common objections from your ideal customer
6. Talk about systems unique to your business
7. Tour of the workplace
8. Interviews
9. Bloopers
10. Carousel versions of any of the above

**The seven levels** (his ladder of production effort, DcpexBthgLm):

| Level | What | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Photo of finished work as a reel | Do not expect this to work |
| 2 | Video of finished work | Works only if the work is world class |
| 3 | You on camera with the finished work, talking | Where you cap out with no content person |
| 4 | Add captions | Cuts the viewer's mental load |
| 5 | Add detail b-roll | Needs a second person |
| 6 | Add process b-roll | Needs someone there at the exact moment |
| 7 | Add animations and diagrams | Needs design and technical understanding |

Craftons sits at 3 to 4 most of the time. **Level 7 is where we should be winning** and almost nobody in
the trade can reach it: we already generate the geometry. A configurator screen, a nest, a setout diagram
is a level 7 asset produced as a by-product of the job.

## 6. Repeat the winner

Their curved cabinet doors have been posted about a dozen times: 1.3M, 680K, 400K, 300K views, and the
latest is still at half a million. Different angle each time. How it was built. FAQs from the comments.
The build process.

> "Your audience isn't going to hate you for it in the same way that you don't hate your favourite artist
> for playing their greatest hit at each of their concerts."

**Craftons' curved doors are curved formwork.** Radius Pro is the repeatable winner and we have been
treating hits as one-offs. When one lands, do not move on. Attack it from every angle for months.

### The caveat he leaves out, and it matters

The account data does not support "repeat the topic and the result repeats". It repeats the topic
relentlessly (33 of 180 posts involve curves; the GPO drawer detail appears in 8 separate posts) and the
results swing wildly:

- The same GPO detail across 8 posts: **124 to 4,680 likes.**
- Two near-identical curved-door builds, almost the same opening line: **5,366 versus 507.**

**The topic buys a shot. The structure and the hook decide whether it lands.** Repeat the *format that
worked*, not merely the subject. Following his version literally produces a lot of 124-like posts about
proven topics. Read this next to §1: the 24x pair were both about the same kind of cabinetry.

The test before repeating: does it resonate with the ideal customer, does it build the brand rather than
cheapen it, and can you find a genuinely new angle rather than restating the last one.

## 7. Filler-day tactics

For the weeks the workshop is flat out and nobody films. He claims 1,000 followers on a day with nothing
new to post, using three moves:

1. **Straight repost of anything over 90 days old.**
2. **Alternate takes of past winners.** Go back through the rushes of a video that worked.
3. **Trial reel versions** of existing videos.

Cheap insurance. Set it up before it is needed, not during.

## 8. Extracting ideas from one job

The full interview prompt is on Drive at
`01 Inspiration/jade.ryuki/skool-30-days-content-prompt.md` — copy it out and run it verbatim when you
want 30 ideas in ten minutes. **Jake answers, not Lee.** Steps 4 to 7 need the person who made it, with
documentation to hand.

The short version, when you just need a shot list for one job:

1. **One project.** A real job from the last 12 to 24 months with photos, video, drawings or data.
2. **Go wide on details.** List 5 to 10 specific things *within* it. Not the project, the pieces inside.
   A junction. A radius. A material. A fixing. A particular dimension. Breadth, not depth.
3. **Process.** Across those details, what did it actually take that an outsider would not realise?
4. **Problems faced.** What went wrong and how was it solved?
5. **Problems avoided.** What did not go wrong because of a system already in place. **This is usually the
   richest seam for Craftons and the one nobody else can answer.** The configurator caught it at
   quoting. The nest sorted it. The part IDs meant no guessing on site. The setout meant no measuring.
6. **Frameworks.** The "we always do X because Y" rules of thumb.

Then spread the output across five types, roughly even: finished outcome, how it was made, problems
solved, problems avoided by systems, repeatable rules.

**Every idea must trace to something real that was said.** No invented details, no generic "5 things
builders should know". One concrete line per idea, so the reader knows immediately what to film.

Strong: *"The 2.4m radius wall where not one panel was cut on site."*
Weak: *"How we make curved formwork."*

## 9. Critiquing a draft

Run a script or a hook against these, in order. Fail any of the first three and it gets rewritten, not
tweaked.

1. **Is it one idea, or is it a tour?** If it lists what a job contains, it is a tour and it will lose.
   Cut it into separate videos, one detail each. Measured cost of getting this wrong on an otherwise
   identical post: **24x** (§1). Most common fault, biggest single win.
2. **Does it give the reason, not just the feature?** "We did X" loses. "The reason we did X is Y" wins.
   The best-performing post in the whole dataset says "the reason" three times in four sentences.
3. **Does the first line name a specific problem?** Generic opening, generic result. A number, a dimension
   or a constraint in the first two seconds.
4. **Does it close on cost, programme or risk?** Not on how clever we are.
5. **Are the dead ends in there?** If it jumps straight to the answer, it lost the trust-building middle.
6. **Is the problem shown, not just narrated?** B-roll, diagram, or the part in hand.
7. **Is it for the customer or for the trade?** A reel that will thrill other fabricators and sell nothing
   is the trap. Check who it flatters.
8. **Does it open with the content?** No "welcome back", no logo sting, no brand intro. Across 148 Plot
   Joinery posts there is not one.
9. **Would a builder believe every technical claim?** Anything uncertain goes back to Jake before it ships.
10. **Voice check.** Hand to `craftons-voice`. No em dashes, no "precision", Australian spelling, outcomes
    before mechanism.

## 10. Captions

His practice, and our master synthesis independently agrees: **tight captions, no hashtag dump.** His own
median is 42 characters with zero hashtags across 28 posts. The video carries everything; the caption is
a title card.

Plot Joinery confirms it at scale: **45-character median caption, one hashtag across 148 posts.**

Craftons variant: keep it short, make it a title or a plain statement of the outcome, and route softly to
the configurator. Do not restate the video. Do not stack hashtags.

### The numbered case-study library — their best system, and we do not have it

This is the biggest structural finding from the client account and he never mentions it in his own content.

Their captions are almost always one line: **"Comment 103 to watch our full HVAC grilles case study."**
Numbers observed in a single quarter: 100 to 104, 112 to 119. So there is an **indexed back catalogue of
long-form case studies**, and every short-form post is a trailer pointing at one by number. Short-form earns
the reach; the gate turns it into a DM conversation.

The effect, measured over 148 posts:

| | Posts | Median likes | Median comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gated | 37 | **498** | **46** |
| Ungated | 111 | 175 | 2 |

**2.8x the likes and 23x the comments.** Part of the likes gap is that they gate their best material, but
the comment gap is pure mechanism, and comments are the strongest ranking signal on the platform.

**What this means for us:** the gate is worthless without a library to point at, and the library is a year
of groundwork. Start now. One proper long-form case study per significant job, numbered, and every
short-form post about that job points at it. Until the library exists, use gates sparingly and only where
the payoff is real. A gate with a weak payoff trains people to ignore the next one.

## 10b. Where we deliberately differ from Plot Joinery

Copy the mechanics, not the business model.

- **They never mention price, lead time or how to order. Not once in 148 posts.** They are a bespoke shop
  selling craft, and the enquiry is the conversion. Craftons sells a productised, configurable,
  dispatched-in-days offer, and the configurator is the wedge. **We close on the mechanism where they close
  on the detail.** Soft route to configure, every time, without turning the video into an ad.
- **They have a founder on camera in a workshop daily.** We have two sites and nobody holding a camera.
  That is the real constraint on copying them, and it is a resourcing decision, not a content one.
- **Systems content is open to us and closed to them.** They cannot make "configure it online and it arrives
  cut to size". Pillar 4 in the master synthesis, and still our unfair advantage.

## 11. What this skill will not do

- Invent a technical detail, a dead end, or a tolerance. Ask Jake.
- Produce "content about content". That is Jade's niche, not ours. We sell building products.
- Recommend trends, dances or skits. He is explicit that you do not need them and neither do we.
- Chase reach that flatters the trade instead of reaching buyers.
- Ship words to a customer without `craftons-voice`.
