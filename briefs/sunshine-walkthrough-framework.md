## 3. Audio — the mic-pointing dip ✅ fixable, and fixed

**What happens:** the mic is the handheld wireless transmitter. Every time Lee points, it moves away from his
mouth. Measured on 6172:

| Section | Speech level |
|---|---|
| Talking, mic near mouth (0:05–0:18) | about −33 dBFS |
| Pointing (0:20–0:40, 0:48–1:30) | about −45 to −50 dBFS, **a 12–15 dB drop** |
| Room between words | about −58 to −60 dBFS |

The voice stays 10–15 dB above the room, so it's recoverable. No voiceover needed.

**Fix:** speech-aware gain riding (`pipeline/level-speech.py`). It finds the words with Whisper, lifts
only those stretches (up to +18 dB, smoothed over 250 ms so there's no pumping), and leaves the gaps
alone, so footsteps and room tone don't come up with it. Result on 6172 and 6173: speech now sits
consistently at **−23 to −27 dBFS** the whole way through, and gaps stay around −55.

A plain compressor/normaliser was tried first and rejected: it boosted the silent gaps by 40 dB
(footsteps and hiss as loud as the voice).

**Residual:** a few long pauses inside sentences (6172 0:59–1:04, 1:22–1:25) come up to about −39 dBFS of room
tone. All of them are already cut in the framework above.

Final mix target for social: −14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP.
