## 4. The method, as observed elsewhere (teardown)

Nobody in NZ markets this. The approach is real and running overseas — proof the engineering works.

**Sapiens Routing / CNC Cutting Services (Canada)** — closest confirmed match to Craftons' concept.
Sells it as a service: CAD in, **CNC-routed plywood formwork plates** out, "clean edges, accurate
dimensions, perfect repeatability." The operational unlock: **every part carries a unique part
number**, so crews assemble without drawings. That numbering is what turns a pile of ply into
something a labourer builds right first time.

**PERI (global)** — runs a formal **"CNC Cutting and Formwork Assembly"** service: cut-to-size ply
panels and "special formwork of all kinds — from simple formats and box outs to **3D formwork units
for once-only applications**." The incumbent proving there's a real market in machine-cut one-offs.

**Digital Mould Design (Australia)** — complex/compound-curve formwork, Australia-wide. Laser-cut
steel, CNC-machined and hot-wire-cut EPS, fibreglass, 3D printing. Mould sections built in
**handleable sizes that lock or bolt together on site** to make runs of any length.

**Space Craft Systems / WikiHouse NZ (Christchurch)** — wrong application, right joint. CNC ply
parts as "3D jigsaw pieces that slot together", assembled **with a mallet, no power tools, by
unskilled people**, from 19mm structural ply. That is precisely the labour argument Craftons is
making, already proven in NZ on plywood.

**Generic method, per multiple sources:** internal ply ribs stop the form bulging under pressure,
conventionally at **300–600mm** spacing from 12–16mm ply; AU/NZ wall forming standard is 17mm F17
formply at 400–600mm stud spacing. Craftons' 25mm BC at wider spacing is a stiffer variant of a
known approach. **The novelty isn't the rib — it's keying it into a machined plate so the panel
self-jigs instead of being measured and nailed.** Joint vocabulary for that (trapezoidal wedge tabs
with press-fit roots, dog-bones at internal corners) is well catalogued in CNC panel-joinery
references. Nobody found anywhere is productising 25mm-ply-soldier-into-machined-plate — **which is
the actually interesting finding for Craftons.**
