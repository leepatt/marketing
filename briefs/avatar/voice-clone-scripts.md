# Presenter voice — clone source script + bake-off test script

## How to use
Generate the CLONE SOURCE in Google AI Studio (Generate speech) in 3-4 chunks, same voice +
style prompt every chunk, download WAV. QC on headphones, regenerate bad chunks. Upload to
HeyGen -> Voices -> Create voice clone as `Craftons-Presenter-Voice-v1`.

**Locked style prompt (fill in once chosen):**
`Speak in a natural Australian accent, relaxed mid-pace, warm and matter-of-fact, like a
tradesman explaining something he knows well.`
Voice name: ______  Model: ______  Date locked: ______

## CLONE SOURCE SCRIPT (~4 min, generate in the chunks marked)

### Chunk 1 — calm explanation (~70s)
Curved walls used to be the hard part of any build. You'd either pay a specialist joiner,
or you'd stand there kerfing sheets by hand and hoping nothing split. That's the problem we
set out to fix. Every panel is cut on our CNC machines in Melbourne, from real CAD geometry,
so the curve you order is the curve that turns up. No guesswork, no site adjustments, no
surprises. You measure once, you order once, and it fits. That's the whole idea. It's not
complicated, it's just done properly, and honestly that's rarer than it should be.

### Chunk 2 — enthusiasm + numbers (~70s)
This one came out of the workshop yesterday and it's a ripper. A curved bench seat, three
point six metres end to end, cut from eighteen mil form ply in fourteen segments. The radius?
Nine hundred and fifty millimetres, dead consistent the whole way round. Standard sheets are
twenty-four hundred by twelve hundred, and we nested the whole job into just five of them.
Five! The offcut bin barely noticed. The builder had it assembled before smoko. Two people,
a drill, forty minutes. That's what happens when the cutting is right the first time.

### Chunk 3 — question, listening, direct address (~60s)
So here's the question we get every single week. Can you actually ship a curve? Fair
question. Curves sound fragile. But here's the thing — everything ships flat. The segments
lock together on site, like a big, very accurate puzzle. So yes, it goes on a standard
pallet, on a standard truck, to Mornington, to Geelong, to wherever you're building. What's
the tightest radius we can do? Tighter than you'd think. Send us the drawing and we'll tell
you straight. If it can't be done, we'll say so, and we'll usually suggest what will work.

### Chunk 4 — serious, measured, trust (~60s)
One thing we won't do is pretend. If a detail won't survive the real world — moisture,
movement, a job site that doesn't read the instructions — we'll tell you before we cut it.
Formwork has to hold concrete. Architraves have to sit flush. An MDF profile that looks
perfect in a render can still fail at the join if nobody thought about the substrate. So we
think about it. Every file gets checked by a person before the machine ever starts. That's
the standard. It costs us a bit of time. It saves you a lot more.

## BAKE-OFF TEST SCRIPT (~45s — render on the avatar with BOTH voices)
G'day. Quick one on the curved architraves for the Mornington job. They're cut from
eighteen mil MDF on the CNC, radius matched to your drawing, and they ship flat from our
Melbourne workshop — same as the Radius Pro panels and the formwork. Sheet size is
twenty-four hundred by twelve hundred, tolerance under a millimetre. If you're out near
Geelong, freight's usually a day. Send the CAD file, or even a photo of the opening, and
Craftons will take it from there. Easy as.

### Listen for
- [ ] Accent holds Australian the whole way (worst spots: "data", "radius", "here's")
- [ ] Craftons / architrave / CNC / MDF / formwork / Mornington / Geelong all clean
- [ ] Numbers read naturally (eighteen mil, twenty-four hundred by twelve hundred)
- [ ] Pace varies — chunk 2 energy vs chunk 4 weight
- [ ] No robotic warble, no gargled syllables, no American vowel creep
